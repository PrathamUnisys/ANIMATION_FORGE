# Audio Storytelling Playbook
**Status:** DRAFT. Guidance for the Audio Director.

## Voice and dialogue
Specify delivery where it changes meaning: restrained, uncertain, urgent, intimate, exhausted or controlled. Prioritise intelligibility. Do not narrate an action the viewer can already understand unless the line adds perspective or new information.

## Foley and effects
Use sound to clarify actions or provide evidence: mechanisms, footsteps, material texture, weather, object handling and signature supernatural sounds. Note the sound source, whether the character hears it and whether the audience hears more than the character.

## Music and silence
Use ambient score to support atmosphere. Earn crescendos through story escalation. Silence or a reduction in score can focus attention on an important sound or action. Consider recurring motifs for characters or entities without overusing them.

## Mixing and space
Keep dialogue intelligible over music and effects. Document near/far and inside/outside perspective where relevant. Avoid spatial or loudness effects that make essential dialogue or clues inaudible.

## Screenplay cue style
- `AUDIO: low room tone; distant metal vibration.`
- `MUSIC: score drops out as the hand reaches the latch.`
- `SFX: one bell strike, heard from behind camera.`
- `VOICE: whispered with recognition, not panic.`

These cues guide design; they are not a complete mix specification.
