# Titles, Thumbnails and Packaging Playbook
**Status:** DRAFT. Human selection required.

## Purpose
Create a clear reason to click while accurately representing the viewing experience delivered by the episode.

## Promise alignment
For each title/thumbnail pairing ask:
1. What question or emotional promise does it create?
2. Is that promise genuinely present in the episode?
3. Does the opening establish the same subject or mystery?
4. Does the episode fulfil the expectation?
5. Could the wording or image imply a person, event or outcome that is not in the video?

Reject options that rely on misleading implications.

## Titles
The source report suggests intriguing, specific phrases; question titles where a central question exists; natural genre/topic terms; and avoidance of sensational claims the story cannot support. Generate multiple candidates with different angles and compare clarity, distinctiveness and promise alignment. Do not force fixed keyword counts or title lengths.

## Thumbnails
The report suggests one clear main subject, strong contrast, readable hierarchy, evocative story-consistent lighting/colour and minimal text where it helps. Maintain a recurring series identity without sacrificing episode-specific clarity. The report's 3–4-word text suggestion is optional, not a hard limit.

## Testing
Where platform tools and sufficient data are available, record variants, test context and dates. Change one major variable at a time where practical. Review click behaviour together with post-click viewing. Do not claim a causal winner from weak or confounded evidence.

## Deliverable
Provide 3–5 title options and 2–3 thumbnail concepts as an initial editorial workflow. A human chooses the published package.
