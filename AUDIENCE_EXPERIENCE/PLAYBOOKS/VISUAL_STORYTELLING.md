# Visual Storytelling Playbook
**Status:** DRAFT. Animation-aware craft guidance.

## Composition and shot intent
- Use wide/establishing shots when viewers need location or spatial context.
- Use medium shots for interaction and readable body language.
- Use close-ups for emotional shifts and important clues.
- Use extreme close-ups sparingly when a detail must be noticed.
- Keep the visual subject and story information legible.

These are shot functions, not quotas.

## Camera movement
- Use slow push-ins or pans to guide attention toward discovery or emotional change.
- Reserve aggressive motion for moments where urgency or instability is part of the story.
- Do not add camera movement just to make every shot appear busy.
- Preserve spatial continuity when clue geography matters.

## Lighting, colour and motifs
The source report suggests candlelight/chiaroscuro for Occult Detective; earthy palettes for Dark Folklore when culturally and narratively appropriate; high contrast and selected unnatural colours for Cosmic Horror. Treat these as options, not rigid visual codes.

Introduce important objects and symbols clearly before changing their meaning later. A recurring motif should serve the story.

## Character animation
Prioritise readable expressions, eye direction, gestures and posture. Small actions such as a blink, head turn or hand hesitation can carry emotion. Keep eyelines and prop contact consistent.

## Production effort tiers
| Shot class | Purpose | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| Narrative-critical | Clue, decision, reveal or emotional turn | Highest attention and clarity |
| Mood-supporting | Atmosphere, location or tension | Moderate, controlled detail |
| Transitional | Bridge between story moments | Efficient and reusable |

## Storyboard handoff
For critical panels record what viewers must notice, action/eyeline, important props, composition, lighting where relevant, required motion, optional ambient motion and continuity constraints. Visuals should add information rather than merely repeat dialogue.
