# Screenwriting Constitution — Proposal

> **Status: DRAFT PROPOSAL.** Advisory design material only. It is not an active global instruction and does not supersede approved canon, project briefs, genre profiles, rights restrictions, or existing governance.

## Purpose

Define a consistent, model-independent standard for compelling, coherent, emotionally credible, visually producible animated stories. The standard guides decisions without prescribing one formula for every genre or format. The practical process is in PLAYBOOKS/SCREENPLAY_ENGINEERING.md.

## Authority

Apply the existing authority model: system/security/application permissions; global governance; approved project brief and restrictions; approved canon; genre; series format and channel style; this creative standard and relevant playbooks; then department, agent, provider, and task instructions.

This proposal cannot override approved canon, project brief, genre, rights restrictions, or human approval policy. Audience Experience is cross-cutting guidance, not a new authority tier.

## Creative principles

### Audience promise and story identity
- State the intended experience in one clear sentence.
- Ensure premise, opening, progression, packaging proposal, and ending support the same promise.
- Treat audience and format assumptions as explicit inputs.
- Prefer a distinctive, comprehensible dramatic engine over accumulated lore or arbitrary twists.

### Character agency and motivation
- Make the protagonist's objective, stakes, and meaningful choices understandable.
- Give major characters objectives and motives legible through action or context.
- Decisions should follow established motivation or a supported change.
- Opposition should have a comprehensible motive, even when morally wrong.
- Emotional turns should arise from shown events and relationships.

### Causality and coherence
- Important events follow from established conditions, choices, or introduced rules.
- Major turns need understandable causal bridges.
- Distinguish coincidence that starts a story from coincidence that conveniently resolves its conflict.
- Track unresolved questions, contradictions, and assumptions.
- A twist changes interpretation without making earlier scenes dishonest.

### Hook and fulfilment
- The opening creates a relevant question, tension, emotional connection, or striking image.
- The hook connects to the actual story and receives meaningful payoff.
- Avoid unrelated shock, false stakes, and artificial interruptions.
- No universal hook deadline is mandated.

### Setup, clues, rules, and reveals
- Track important promises, objects, clues, relationships, and rules through setup and payoff.
- A deduction is supported by information available to the audience or protagonist at the relevant time.
- Distinguish clue, red herring, inference, reveal, and confirmation.
- Establish supernatural, technological, or magical rules before they become decisive.
- A reveal answers the central question and accounts for important evidence.
- Apply mystery requirements only when relevant to the selected story engine.

### Scene progression
Each scene performs one or more functions: advance an objective, transform conflict, reveal information, change a relationship, alter emotion, establish a rule, deliver a payoff, or create a necessary transition. Quiet scenes are valid when meaningful or intentional.

### Dialogue, image, and sound
- Dialogue is speakable, character-specific, and serves action, conflict, relationship, or subtext.
- Do not insert required wording mechanically when the requirement is a story beat rather than exact text.
- Prefer observable action, image, sound, and performance over redundant explanation.
- Camera and animation notes clarify story information or emotion, not decorate.
- Story-critical audio remains intelligible.
- Visual motifs develop meaning; decorative detail is not a substitute for story information.

### Emotional and ending integrity
- Earn emotional responses through setup, choice, consequence, and relationship.
- Resolve the central question to the degree promised by the format.
- Ambiguity is intentional, not missing causal information.
- The final beat expresses consequence, transformation, thematic meaning, or a deliberate series question.

### Production and runtime integrity
- Write filmable action and distinguish essential information from optional embellishment.
- Identify critical props, locations, characters, effects, and audio.
- Flag complexity and unknown dependencies.
- Label runtime as estimate until measured in animatic/final render; record method and assumptions.
- Do not force universal scene counts, beat intervals, or runtime formulas.

## Requirement classification

Use REQUIREMENT_CLASSIFICATION.md. Mandatory beats and clues require their narrative function, not necessarily verbatim wording. Only EXACT_TEXT requires exact reproduction. Split compound requirements into atomic items.

## Artifact traceability

Where supported, link Audience Plan, hook options, packaging options, outline, scene IDs, Retention Map beats, Creative Review findings, and revision lineage. The map describes the actual screenplay revision. Packaging reflects the actual story. Findings cite the exact revision.

Do not claim an artifact is generated, persisted, schema-valid, or linked without evidence.

## Review and revision

Read the screenplay without companion metadata first. Check premise/promise, objective/stakes, motivation, causal spine, clues and rules, scene purpose, dialogue, visual/audio delivery, ending, feasibility, and runtime evidence. Compare map and packaging with the script. Report evidence, interpretation, severity, recommendation, and unchecked areas separately. Revise in priority order and recheck downstream consequences.

## Severity and approval

Use BLOCKER, MAJOR, MINOR, NOTE with evidence and consequence. Creative critique, deterministic validation, technical QC, and human approval remain separate. No score, average, agent recommendation, or technical check can approve a creative deliverable.

## Non-goals and activation

No mandatory three-act structure, fixed twist/cut/cliffhanger interval, universal retention threshold, or average-score gate. No guarantee of retention or commercial performance.

Activation requires a reviewed and approved change request specifying exact files, compatibility decisions, tests, rollback, and agent/schema/workflow integration. Until then this remains advisory design material.
