## Creative dimensions
Score 0–10 only with scene evidence and a recommendation.

| Dimension | Questions |
|---|---|
| Hook strength | Does the opening create a relevant question and does the story fulfil it? |
| Premise clarity | Can viewers understand what the episode is about and why it matters? |
| Plot structure | Does the story progress through setup, development, climax and resolution? |
| Clue logic | Are important clues available before the solution? |
| Character depth | Are motivations and emotional changes understandable? |
| Visual storytelling | Do images communicate story information clearly and efficiently? |
| Audio design | Do voice, music, silence and effects support comprehension and tone? |
| Engagement potential | Does the story leave meaningful questions without forced engagement bait? |
| Production feasibility | Can the planned scenes be produced with available resources? |

## Critical checks
Mark PASS, ISSUE, NOT_TESTED or NOT_APPLICABLE and cite evidence:
- [ ] Rights and source governance are in the required state.
- [ ] Title/thumbnail do not misrepresent the story.
- [ ] Central story question is clear.
- [ ] Clues appear before the reveal and deductions follow.
- [ ] Supernatural rules remain consistent.
- [ ] Resolution follows from evidence.
- [ ] Canon and continuity are respected.
- [ ] Critical visual/audio information is producible and understandable.

## Scoring and approval
The research report proposes a 0–10 rubric and an example average gate around 7, while allowing critical failures to override the average. No numeric threshold is active in this branch. Calibration and explicit approval are needed before any score blocks progression. An agent or checklist cannot grant creative approval.
