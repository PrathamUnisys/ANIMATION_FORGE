# Story Research and Acquisition Workflow

**Status:** Preparatory workflow. No automatic source discovery, legal clearance or screenplay generation is implied.

## Stage 1 — Intake

Capture:
- User's title or working title.
- Short premise or supplied story.
- Desired genre and series format.
- Source route selected by the user, if known.
- Intended adaptation or reference use.
- Target language and publication territories, when known.
- Whether the user supplied source files or links.

If the source route is unknown, ask one focused question or offer clearly labelled options. Do not infer rights status from the title.

## Stage 2 — Discovery and source dossier

Research potential sources using primary or authoritative material where possible. Record the original source separately from secondary commentary and adaptations. Use `TEMPLATES/SOURCE_DOSSIER_TEMPLATE.md`.

For historical mysteries, maintain a fact ledger with source, date, confidence, contradiction and whether each element is documented or disputed.

For folklore, identify the region/community, collection, narrator/collector, variant, publication, translator and differences between versions where available.

## Stage 3 — Rights assessment

Complete `TEMPLATES/RIGHTS_ASSESSMENT_TEMPLATE.md`. Identify exact material, territories and intended use. Record missing evidence. Escalate uncertainty instead of manufacturing a definitive status.

## Stage 4 — Story extraction

Create a source-neutral story analysis:
- Core premise or documented event.
- Main conflict or mystery.
- Protagonist and relevant historical/fictional figures.
- Stakes and consequences.
- Chronology and cause/effect.
- Clues, evidence and competing explanations.
- Themes and atmosphere.
- Elements that are source facts versus editorial interpretation.

Do not copy protected wording or distinctive expression into the extraction unless a defined permission or legal basis has been reviewed.

## Stage 5 — Concept development

Where adaptation is cleared for the intended use, generate adaptation concepts that remain faithful to the selected source scope.

Where the source is inspiration-only, develop independent concepts and run an originality review. Keep the influence references visible to reviewers.

For original user ideas, preserve the user's core intent and label any AI-proposed additions as proposals.

Create separate alternatives rather than blending incompatible ideas into one vague concept. Use `TEMPLATES/STORY_CONCEPT_TEMPLATE.md`.

## Stage 6 — Human selection and approval

Present:
- Source route and dossier.
- Rights/originality status and evidence.
- Three concept alternatives where useful.
- Creative strengths and limitations.
- Production implications.
- Open questions and required decisions.

The user selects and approves a story concept. AI suggestions must not silently become canon.

## Stage 7 — Screenplay handoff

Pass the approved concept, source IDs, permitted-use notes, facts ledger, originality notes, genre IDs, series-format ID, channel style and project constraints to the screenplay context assembler.

Record the source references and versions used. A screenplay approval remains a separate human gate.

## Stage 8 — Post-publication learning

Store audience analytics and editorial observations separately from source and rights records. Performance data may inform future creative choices but does not change source ownership or rights status.
