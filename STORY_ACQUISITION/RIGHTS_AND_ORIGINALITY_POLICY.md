# Rights and Originality Review Policy

**Status:** Preparatory policy draft. Requires review before operational adoption.

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines the questions Animation Forge should record before a story source is adapted or published. It is designed to prevent the system from confusing age, public availability, folklore status, attribution or AI output with permission.

It is not legal advice and must not automatically decide whether a work is legally safe.

## 2. Identify the material precisely

For every source, record as applicable:

- Work title and author/creator.
- Original publication, release or performance date.
- Author/creator death date where relevant and reliably evidenced.
- Country of origin and first publication.
- Exact edition, publisher and publication date.
- Translator, editor, illustrator, photographer and other contributors.
- URL, archive, collection or physical source details.
- Whether the source is a primary record, scholarly edition, later retelling, adaptation, summary or commentary.

Do not assume that the rights status of an original work automatically covers a modern translation, illustration, recording, edition, introduction or adaptation.

## 3. Define intended use

An assessment must describe what the project intends to do:

- Research/reference only.
- Extract historical facts or broad ideas.
- Quote or reproduce limited source material.
- Retell a traditional narrative.
- Adapt a literary work into a screenplay.
- Use source characters, settings or distinctive fictional elements.
- Reproduce illustrations, photographs, audio or other media.
- Publish and monetise an animated video.
- Distribute across named territories, languages and platforms.

A rights assessment is meaningful only against a defined use and territory.

## 4. Territory and term checks

Copyright duration and exceptions vary by jurisdiction and can depend on work type, publication history, authorship, publication country, treaties and other facts.

For a proposed public-domain adaptation, record the legal basis and evidence for every material target territory. Do not infer worldwide public-domain status from one country's term.

A simple year cutoff or author-death lookup may be useful for discovery triage, but it is not final clearance.

## 5. Open licences and permissions

For openly licensed works, capture the exact licence version and check:

- Whether commercial use is permitted.
- Whether adaptations are permitted.
- Attribution and notice requirements.
- Share-alike obligations.
- No-derivatives restrictions.
- Additional rights not covered by the licence.

For licensed or commissioned works, preserve the written agreement and verify that the grant covers the intended media, adaptations, monetisation, territories, languages, duration, sublicensing, promotion and derivative works as needed.

Do not treat an email, informal social post, paid purchase or platform upload as sufficient permission without examining its terms and the sender's authority.

## 6. Originality review for inspiration-only material

The system may record broad themes, genres, historical subjects, general premises and abstract ideas as inspiration inputs. It must not use "inspiration" as a label to conceal copying of distinctive protected expression.

Review proposed concepts for similarities in:

- Distinctive characters and character relationships.
- Specific fictional settings and world rules.
- Recognisable scene sequences and plot combinations.
- Distinctive dialogue or descriptive language.
- Original invented creatures, objects, organisations or symbols.
- Signature reveals or highly specific combinations of events.

This review is a risk-screening process, not a legal conclusion. Escalate close cases.

## 7. AI-assisted original creation

Record the human-provided premise, creative decisions, selected alternatives, revisions and material human contributions where appropriate. Check the relevant AI provider's terms and applicable law. Do not claim that every AI-generated element is automatically copyright-protected or automatically free of third-party rights.

## 8. Clearance gate

Recommended future statuses:

- `NOT_ASSESSED`
- `RESEARCH_IN_PROGRESS`
- `REVIEW_REQUIRED`
- `CLEARED_FOR_DEFINED_USE`
- `RESTRICTED_USE`
- `PERMISSION_REQUIRED`
- `REJECTED`
- `UNKNOWN`

Only `CLEARED_FOR_DEFINED_USE` may pass the future adaptation gate, and only for the documented use and territories. This status must be supported by evidence and an authorised human review. It must not be generated from a model's unsupported conclusion.

## 9. Evidence retention

Preserve source URLs, access dates, saved bibliographic details, licence/permission files, assessment notes, reviewer identity, decision date, territories, intended use, limitations and unresolved issues. Keep source material and permission evidence separate from generated screenplay content.

## 10. Mandatory escalation

Escalate when:
- The rights holder or authorship is uncertain.
- A work has multiple editions, translations or adaptations with unclear provenance.
- Target territories have conflicting or unclear rules.
- A proposed concept closely resembles a modern copyrighted work.
- Licence terms are ambiguous or incomplete.
- The permission grant does not clearly cover animation, online distribution or commercial use.
- Personal, confidential or sensitive source material is involved.

Do not publish or adapt while a material rights blocker remains unresolved.
