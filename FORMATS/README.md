# Series Formats

## Purpose

Series formats define how stories and episodes are organised and connected. They are separate from genre and channel style.

- **Genre:** What narrative conventions and audience expectations govern the story?
- **Series format:** How do episodes relate, repeat, resolve or accumulate?
- **Channel style:** How does a publishing brand present its videos and communicate its audience promise?

The same genre can support multiple series formats. The same format can be used with different genres.

## Initial format proposal

The provisional first format is `FORMAT_SHARED_UNIVERSE_ANTHOLOGY`: primarily self-contained episodes or cases within a shared fictional universe, with optional recurring characters, locations, mythology and longer arcs.

It remains a draft until its conventions are tested against a pilot and approved.

## Requirements for a format profile

Each profile should describe:
- Format promise and unit of storytelling.
- Episode independence and continuity.
- Recurring characters and locations.
- Season/series arc options.
- Entry points for new viewers.
- Episode ending and transition conventions.
- Reusable asset implications.
- Common structural failures.
- Review checklist.
- Compatibility with genre profiles and channel style.

Avoid hardcoding runtime, release cadence or retention tactics into the format profile unless the channel owner explicitly configures them.
