## Story comprehension (Pass A)
- **What it’s about:** A sincerity-metric bureaucracy meets a citizen demanding apology for never being wronged; the clerk finds a truthful apology for the institution’s habit rather than a fake injury.
- **Central audience question (brief):** “Can an apology be real when grievance is not?”
- **Protagonist:** Noor, junior clerk, wants to close the file sincerely within a system that quantifies remorse.
- **Stakes:** Escalation to a ministry-wide apology for “absence of nothing”; more deeply, the erasure of real wrongs on **the unread complaints shelf** while performative procedure consumes attention.
- **Opposition:** **The sincerity audit** / Auditor Vell’s metric logic and form constraints; Mr. B’s insistence; Noor’s own role in a stamping machine.
- **Change by ending:** Noor names the true harm (being noticed only when injured), Vell files an unscorable habit-entry, Noor creates a “HABITS” tray and returns to real work with purpose.
- **Intended feeling/understanding:** Satirical recognition + a small, honest human connection; systems can be bent toward meaning without pretending harm occurred.

## Strengths to preserve
- Core satirical premise and escalation (SC01→SC03) is clear and funny.
- Visual/prop logic is strong: **the apology form 7B**, **the unread complaints shelf**, **the audit's sincerity score** dial.
- SC04 turn (“I want… to be seen. …quiet, being fine.”) is the emotional heart — keep and sharpen.
- Ending image (HABITS tray, return to a real hurt) pays off Noor’s decency and the shelf contrast.
- All required beats/clues appear verbatim: **the Ministry of Small Apologies**; **the grievance that never happened**; **the sincerity audit**; **the apology form 7B**; **the unread complaints shelf**; **the audit's sincerity score**.
