# EP10 Opening Options (Pass I)

**Episode:** EP10 — *The Ministry of Small Apologies* | **Improved revision:** `SCRIPTS/EP10_screenplay.md` (framework-upgrade, 2026-11-10)  
**Central question/promise:** Can an apology be real when the grievance never happened? (Can sincerity survive a metric?)  
**Required beat present in world:** `the Ministry of Small Apologies`.

## Option 1 — “Stamp, Stamp, Shelf” (bureaucratic rhythm vs ignored real hurts) — **SELECTED**
- **Opening image/action/sound:** Close on Noor’s stamp falling in rhythm (AUDIO: stamp-thud, printer whine, clock tick). Pull back to reveal **the Ministry of Small Apologies** desk and, in the same frame behind her, **the unread complaints shelf** (tagged REAL file). Noor’s eyes flick to it; queue buzzer pulls her back.
- **Question/emotional connection:** Why does this clerk care about a shelf no one opens — and what happens when “sorry” is just a stamp? Viewer feels weary absurdity + a flicker of conscience.
- **What the audience understands:** Setting (apology bureaucracy), tone (dry satire), Noor’s motive seed (real hurts waiting), stakes of attention.
- **Connection to story:** Directly sets up SC02 contrast (shelf vs Mr. B’s claim) and SC05 payoff (Noor returns to the real file/HABITS tray).
- **Fulfilment beat:** SC05 — Noor places the habit notation in a separate tray and pulls the tagged real file, stamping it “the real kind.” Promise fulfilled: sincerity redirected to real harm.
- **Why selected:** Most honest to actual story; plants motive and visual motif early; no fake stakes; comedic rhythm emerges from truth of system.

## Option 2 — “The Empty Hurt” (Mr. B in the queue)
- **Opening image/action/sound:** Mr. B alone in a long queue, impeccably dressed, rehearsing his complaint under his breath while others clutch broken umbrellas, eviction notices, bandaged arms. He smooths a pristine, empty folder.
- **Question/emotional connection:** What does a man with no visible wound want from an apology office? Curiosity mixed with discomfort.
- **What the audience understands:** Mr. B’s need to be processed; contrast with real grievances (previews shelf).
- **Connection to story:** Motivates SC01 demand and foreshadows SC04 reveal (“quiet, being fine”).
- **Fulfilment beat:** SC04 — Mr. B admits he wants to be seen; SC05 he accepts being spoken to without being asked what was broken.
- **Why not selected:** Strong character intro but delays establishing **the Ministry of Small Apologies** as system/antagonist and Noor’s POV; risks centering Mr. B as oddity before the institutional satire is clear.

## Option 3 — “The Dial” (metric as god)
- **Opening image/action/sound:** Extreme close-up of **the sincerity audit** dial twitching, searching for a number as Vell’s gloved hand feeds it phrases. It whines, fails, resets. Pull back to the audit room poster: “IF YOU CAN’T MEASURE IT, YOU DON’T MEAN IT.”
- **Question/emotional connection:** Can anything human survive a machine that scores remorse? Dread/comic unease.
- **What the audience understands:** The audit mechanism and **the audit's sincerity score** as obstacle; stakes of measurability.
- **Connection to story:** Previews SC03/SC05 confrontation and the filing problem.
- **Fulfilment beat:** SC05 — dial cannot register the margin; Vell logs under 7B-Precedent without a sincerity value (truth filed outside the metric).
- **Why not selected:** Vivid but front-loads the audit before audience meets Noor’s desire or **the grievance that never happened**; would make opening promise more “beat the machine” than “can apology be real,” slightly misaligning with emotional core.

**Selection rationale (summary):** Option 1 best balances premise clarity, protagonist motive, satirical tone, and a promise the ending fulfils (attention redirected to real hurt). Options 2 and 3 remain viable alternates for future cuts if pacing tests favour character-first or mechanism-first entry. No opening uses unrelated shock, fake stakes, or misleading promise.
