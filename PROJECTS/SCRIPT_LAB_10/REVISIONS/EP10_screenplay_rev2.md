FADE IN:

SC01
**INT. MINISTRY APOLOGIES DESK - DAY**

*A vast, beige office of stamps, queues, and sighs. Banners repeat the motto until it means nothing: “SORRY, PROPERLY.” A wall clock ticks like a metronome for remorse.*

*VISUAL (narrative-critical): the camera finds NOOR (20s, junior clerk, sincere, weary) at a desk island. In the same frame, behind her shoulder, looms **the unread complaints shelf** — a towering, dust-edged archive of files no one has opened. Noor’s eyes flick to it between stamps; one folder edge is tagged REAL — WATER DAMAGE, a small private reminder of why she cares.*

*AUDIO: stamp-thud rhythm, printer whine, a queue murmur. MUSIC: dry, minimal, under the machinery.*

SENIOR CLERK (O.S.)  
*(flat, practiced)*  
Bus delay, stamp. Rude weather forecast, stamp. Existential Tuesday, stamp — but only if they ticked box C.

*Noor stamps with mechanical mercy, then pauses on the tagged file as if to pull it — the queue buzzer chirps. Duty wins. She slides it back, jaw tight.*

*MR. B (50s, immaculate coat, wounded dignity) steps forward. He has been waiting a long time; he smooths his folder, straightens his cuffs — a ritual of being processed. He sets the folder down with ceremonial care.*

MR. B  
I am here to be apologised to. Personally.

NOOR  
*(professional warmth, tired)*  
Of course, sir. Welcome to **the Ministry of Small Apologies**. Please describe the injury so I can select the correct form.

MR. B  
That is the injury. You have never apologised to me. Not once. In my entire life.

*Noor blinks. She scrolls categories on her screen — delays, damages, discourtesies. Nothing fits.*

NOOR  
Sir… have we wronged you?

MR. B  
Not that I can recall. But the absence is glaring. I demand redress for **the grievance that never happened**.

*A beat. Noor’s glance catches **the unread complaints shelf** again — real harms, silent. Mr. B waits, expectant, almost hopeful. Against training, a laugh dies in her throat; she chooses care over dismissal.*

*VISUAL: Noor reaches, past the easy forms, to a slim drawer. She pulls **the apology form 7B** — “Miscellaneous / Unprecedented.” The paper looks too small for the problem.*

NOOR  
Then… we’ll have to do this properly.

CUT TO:

SC02
**INT. MINISTRY APOLOGIES DESK - DAY (LATER)**

*The queue has grown. A printer jams sympathetically, coughs, jams again. Noor attempts to complete **the apology form 7B** while Mr. B watches like a hawk, hands folded — present, insistent, oddly polite in his persistence.*

*VISUAL (narrative-critical): Noor turns the form to read the small footer. A printed clause is legible in close-up: **7B-PRECEDENT — when no citizen injury is claimed and escalation review is recorded, log the conduct without a remorse value.** She traces the two conditions with her finger; it is not an apology, but a defined way to record conduct without inventing harm.*

SENIOR CLERK (O.S.)  
Seventh floor logs these under 7B-PRECEDENT: no injury claimed, escalation review recorded. No number, just a record. Takes forever, but it counts as processed.

NOOR  
“Nature of harm…” I can’t write ‘none’ in a harm field. The field gets upset.

MR. B  
Write ‘emotional omission.’ I have felt un-apologised-to in a general, civic sense.

NOOR  
That’s not a— *(types anyway, careful)* —fine. “Responsible party…” the Ministry?

MR. B  
The whole concept of you. But start with your desk.

*VISUAL (narrative-critical): Noor turns slightly; **the unread complaints shelf** fills the background, heavy with unopened lives. She lowers her voice.*

NOOR  
Sir, there are people on that shelf who were actually wronged. I want to help you — but I don’t want to pretend they aren’t waiting.

MR. B  
*(a flicker, quickly masked)*  
And I am sure they’ve had their turn. I have been patient for decades of non-incident.

*He taps the folder once — not anger, a need to be kept in the process. Noor hears the loneliness under the entitlement and doesn’t know what to do with it.*

*She stamps, un-stamps, re-stamps. The apology form 7B begins to look like a confession written in circles. The clock ticks louder.*

*A chime: FILE FLAGGED FOR REVIEW. Noor exhales.*

NOOR  
They’re sending us to audit.

MR. B  
*(relieved, as if escalation is attention)*  
Good. Someone should see this.

CUT TO:

SC03
**INT. MINISTRY AUDIT ROOM - DAY**

*A smaller, colder room. On the wall: **the sincerity audit** — “IF YOU CAN’T MEASURE IT, YOU DON’T MEAN IT.” A large dial apparatus sits like a judge. AUDITOR VELL (40s, crisp) stands by it, tablet in hand.*

*VISUAL (narrative-critical): the dial face is marked 0–10 with a needle that twitches at words like “injury” and “remedy.”*

AUDITOR VELL  
Clerk Noor. You have filed a draft apology for **the grievance that never happened**. Under Procedure 4, an unscorable claim may trigger escalation review. Explain.

NOOR  
The citizen requests acknowledgement of never having been wronged by us. I attempted to respond without inventing harm.

AUDITOR VELL  
Kindness is not a metric. **The sincerity audit** requires a countable injury, a proportional remedy, and a remorse value — the audit’s sincerity score — between 1 and 10.

*Vell turns the dial input. The needle hovers, confused, near zero — it cannot find purchase. She taps RECORD ESCALATION REVIEWED on her tablet; the review status appears beside the case number. AUDIO: a thin, uncertain whine from the device.*

MR. B  
I knew it. Even your remorse is underfunded.

AUDITOR VELL  
If we cannot score it, we cannot issue it. Unresolved, it escalates: a ministry-wide apology for systemic non-apology, auto-generated by volume. Do you understand the scale of that?

NOOR *(horrified, to Mr. B and Vell both)*  
A ministry-wide apology… for nothing?

AUDITOR VELL  
For the absence of nothing, properly formatted. Revise. Produce a scorable injury or close the file. You have until end of queue.

*Vell’s tone is not cruel — procedural, immovable. The dial settles back to rest. Noor gathers **the apology form 7B**; her grip tightens.*

CUT TO:

SC04
**INT. MINISTRY APOLOGIES DESK - DAY (CRUNCH TIME)**

*Back at the desk island, harsh afternoon light. The queue has thinned; the clock looms. Mr. B hovers, less triumphant now, more afraid of being dismissed. The printer coughs out another blank apology form 7B.*

*Noor stares at **the unread complaints shelf**, then at Mr. B, then at the form. She sets her stamp down — a small rebellion of attention.*

NOOR  
Mr. B. What do you actually want? Not the form. You.

*Silence. AUDIO: the stamp rhythm fades; room tone holds.*

MR. B *(caught off guard, voice dropping)*  
I want… to be seen. Everyone gets a letter, a stamp, a story where they were the point. I have only ever been fine. It’s very quiet, being fine.

*The office noise seems to recede around them. Noor’s posture softens; she sets the form down and listens.*

NOOR  
I can’t apologise for hurting you. I didn’t. And I won’t take words from the people on that shelf to fill your form.

MR. B *(small, honest)*  
I know.

NOOR  
But I can apologise for something true.

*She pulls **the apology form 7B** close and begins to write — not in the harm field, but in the margin the audit never reads. VISUAL: her pen moves slowly, deliberately; Mr. B leans in.*

NOOR (CONT’D)  
“We apologise for building a place where you must be injured to be noticed.”

*Mr. B reads it. His posture changes — less triumphant, more human. He blinks, once.*

MR. B  
You can’t stamp that.

NOOR  
No. But I can mean it. And I can ask them to record that we meant it.

*Mr. B nods — not victory, recognition.*

CUT TO:

SC05
**INT. MINISTRY AUDIT ROOM - DAY**

*Noor presents the revised file. Vell scans the scored fields — empty — then the margin. The dial sits idle.*

AUDITOR VELL  
The margin does not register on **the audit's sincerity score**. There is no injury value, no remedy, no sincerity score.

NOOR  
I’m not offering a scored apology for **the grievance that never happened**. I’m naming a habit the Ministry can own. The clause on the form — 7B-PRECEDENT. It logs an unscorable conduct notation without an audit’s sincerity score when no citizen injury is claimed and the escalation risk is recorded. Both are true here. I’m asking you to use it, not to invent a remedy.

*Vell pulls up the clause on her tablet; the text matches the form footer shown earlier. She looks from the clause to Noor, then to Mr. B — who stands quietly, without demand.*

AUDITOR VELL  
Precedent logging is discretionary. It creates no remedy metric.

NOOR  
It creates a record that we saw the habit. I will file it as that — and nothing more.

*Mr. B clears his throat, moved but contained.*

MR. B  
I think I accept. It is the first time this building has spoken to me without asking what was broken.

*He looks at the RECEIVED stamp, then at Noor. The small record matters because it will remain after he leaves.*

MR. B  
Someone will have to read it tomorrow.

*A long beat. Vell’s finger hovers over REJECT. The dial gives no help. She exhales, then reaches for the precedent log.*

AUDITOR VELL *(crisp)*  
No injury claimed. Escalation review recorded. The 7B-PRECEDENT conditions are met. Logged as a conduct notation; no audit’s sincerity score assigned. Filed.

*She stamps RECEIVED — NOT SCORED. The sound is different: duller, honest.*

*VISUAL: Noor returns to her desk. She does not place the file on **the unread complaints shelf**. Instead she sets a new, small tray beside it and hand-labels it: HABITS. She slides Mr. B’s notation inside — separate, contained.*

*Mr. B gathers his folder, nods once to Noor — satisfied, seen — and leaves without demanding a parade.*

*Noor looks at **the unread complaints shelf**, then pulls forward the tagged REAL — WATER DAMAGE file she touched in SC01. She picks up a fresh apology form 7B for a case that actually needs one.*

*She clips an ACTIVE QUEUE slip to the water-damage file and places it in the outgoing tray. The file has left the unread shelf and entered the work queue.*

NOOR *(to herself, quiet resolve)*  
One sorry at a time. The real kind.

*AUDIO: stamp-thud returns, but warmer in mix; a single clear stamp on the real file. MUSIC lifts a half-step, then rests.*

*The camera pulls back over **the Ministry of Small Apologies**, humming, absurd, and — for one small moment — almost honest.*

FADE OUT.
