FADE IN:

SC01
**INT. MINISTRY APOLOGIES DESK - DAY**

*A vast, beige office of stamps, queues, and sighs. A banner reads: **the Ministry of Small Apologies** — “Sorry, Properly.” NOOR (20s, junior clerk) sits at a desk piled with forms, stamping with mechanical mercy.*

SENIOR CLERK (O.S.)  
Bus delay, stamp. Rude weather forecast, stamp. Existential Tuesday, stamp — but only if they ticked box C.

*Noor pulls the next file: MR. B (50s, immaculate coat, wounded dignity) steps forward, clutching a folder as if it contains a crime.*

MR. B  
I am here to be apologised to. Personally.

NOOR  
Of course, sir. Please describe the injury so I can select the correct form.

MR. B  
That is the injury. You have never apologised to me. Not once. In my entire life.

*Noor blinks, searches categories. Nothing fits.*

NOOR  
Sir… have we wronged you?

MR. B  
Not that I can recall. But the absence is glaring. I demand redress for **the grievance that never happened**.

*Noor, against all training, feels a laugh die in her throat. She reaches for **the apology form 7B** — “Miscellaneous / Unprecedented.”*

CUT TO:

SC02
**INT. MINISTRY APOLOGIES DESK - DAY (LATER)**

*Noor attempts to complete **the apology form 7B** while Mr. B watches like a hawk. The queue grows. A printer jams sympathetically.*

NOOR  
“Nature of harm…” I can’t write ‘none’ in a harm field. The field gets upset.

MR. B  
Write ‘emotional omission.’ I have felt un-apologised-to in a general, civic sense.

NOOR  
That’s not a— *(types anyway)* —fine. “Responsible party…” the Ministry?

MR. B  
The whole concept of you. But start with your desk.

*Noor glances at **the unread complaints shelf** behind her — a towering archive of real hurts never opened. The contrast stings: she is being asked to apologise for nothing while everything waits.*

NOOR  
Sir, there are people on that shelf who were actually wronged.

MR. B  
And I am sure they’ve had their turn. I have been patient for decades of non-incident.

*Noor stamps, un-stamps, re-stamps. **The apology form 7B** begins to look like a confession written in circles.*

CUT TO:

SC03
**INT. MINISTRY AUDIT ROOM - DAY**

*Noor is summoned before AUDITOR VELL (40s, measuring sincerity with a handheld dial). Mr. B sits beside her, arms crossed, satisfied to have escalated. A sign on the wall: **the sincerity audit** — “If you can’t measure it, you don’t mean it.”*

AUDITOR VELL  
Clerk Noor, you have filed a draft apology for **the grievance that never happened**. Explain.

NOOR  
The citizen requests acknowledgement of never having been wronged by us. I… attempted to be kind.

AUDITOR VELL  
Kindness is not a metric. **The sincerity audit** requires a countable injury, a proportional remedy, and a remorse value between 1 and 10.

*Vell turns the dial. It hovers, confused, near zero.*

MR. B  
I knew it. Even your remorse is underfunded.

AUDITOR VELL  
If we cannot score it, we cannot issue it. And if we cannot issue it, Mr. B may escalate to a ministry-wide apology for systemic non-apology.

NOOR *(horrified)*  
A ministry-wide apology… for nothing?

AUDITOR VELL  
For the absence of nothing, properly formatted. Please revise.

CUT TO:

SC04
**INT. MINISTRY APOLOGIES DESK - DAY (CRUNCH TIME)**

*Noor returns to her desk, deadline looming. Mr. B looms too. The printer coughs out another blank **apology form 7B**. Noor stares at **the unread complaints shelf**, then at Mr. B, then at the form.*

NOOR  
Mr. B. What do you actually want? Not the form. You.

MR. B *(caught off guard)*  
I want… to be seen. Everyone gets a letter, a stamp, a story where they were the point. I have only ever been fine. It’s very quiet, being fine.

*The office noise fades a fraction. Noor understands: the grievance is loneliness dressed in bureaucracy.*

NOOR  
I can’t apologise for hurting you. I didn’t. But I can apologise for something true.

*She begins to write — not in the harm field, but in the margin the audit never reads.*

NOOR (CONT’D, writing)  
“We apologise for building a place where you must be injured to be noticed.”

*Mr. B reads over her shoulder. His posture changes — less triumphant, more human.*

MR. B  
You can’t stamp that.

NOOR  
No. But I can mean it.

CUT TO:

SC05
**INT. MINISTRY AUDIT ROOM - DAY**

*Noor presents the revised file. Vell scans it, frowns at the unscored margin.*

AUDITOR VELL  
This does not register on **the audit's sincerity score**. There is no injury value.

NOOR  
There is a habit value. I am apologising for the Ministry’s habit of needing a grievance to be sorry — not for Mr. B’s invented injury. I will file it under ‘for the habit itself.’

*Vell’s dial spins, finds nothing, gives up. Mr. B clears his throat, oddly moved.*

MR. B  
I accept. It is… the first time this building has spoken to me without asking what was broken.

AUDITOR VELL *(after a long beat, stamping reluctantly)*  
Unscorable. Therefore unprecedented. Therefore… filed.

*Noor returns to her desk. She places the file not on **the unread complaints shelf**, but in a new, small tray labelled “HABITS.” Mr. B nods, satisfied at last, and leaves without demanding a parade.*

*Noor picks up the next file — a real one, heavy with a real hurt — and pulls a fresh **apology form 7B**. This time, her stamp comes down with quiet purpose.*

NOOR *(to herself)*  
One sorry at a time. The real kind.

*The camera pulls back over **the Ministry of Small Apologies**, humming, absurd, and — for one small moment — almost honest.*

FADE OUT.
