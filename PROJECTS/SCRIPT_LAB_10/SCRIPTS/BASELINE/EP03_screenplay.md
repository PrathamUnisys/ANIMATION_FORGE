# Nine Hours on Redbank Ferry — v2

**Original Story — Approximately 10 Minutes — Corrected v2**

*Visual storytelling over exposition. Dialogue at 120-150 wpm with action and pauses.*

---

FADE IN:

SC01
### EXT. REDBANK FERRY DECK - DAY

**Time: Dawn**

**Characters: ASH, CAPTAIN MARA RUIZ, PASSENGER OMAR, PASSENGERS**

*Visual direction: Practical notebook map in close-up, chalk landing board, and a faint in-camera projection of connecting lines forming over river mist as each landing is named. Ferry horn.*

The REDBANK FERRY pulls away from the pier into grey, low mist. Water slaps the hull. Flat deck, benches, railings, glass pilot-house at the stern.

ASH (19, supervised apprentice ticket-puncher, high-vis vest over life vest, work-permit badge on lanyard) stands with CAPTAIN MARA RUIZ (42, licensed ferry captain, Ash's named supervisor and safety coverage). Mara checks Ash's vest strap, punch, and permit, and signs the morning log.

CAPTAIN MARA: Safety check done. Behind the rail when we're moving, Ash. And rule one — we log fare-payers, we do not question them. Understood?
ASH: Understood. Behind the rail. Log, don't press.

Mara nods and takes the wheel. Ash moves down the line of early passengers, punch in hand. Close-up: ticket-punch snapping.

Ash opens a weatherproof A5 route notebook. Inside, a hand-inked map of the river with landing icons pinned in coloured pencil. This is the living map of landings — built from memory, updated in ink, mirrored on the small chalk board by the gate.

ASH: Mrs. Alvarez — North Landing.
ASH: Two kids — South Landing.
ASH: Mr. Chen — Old Mill Landing.
OMAR: Not today, love. Fog's got my knees. Put me down at Old Mill, I'm walking the short way home.
ASH: Old Mill. Got it — South for the kids, Old Mill for you.

Ash draws a fresh pencil line in the notebook from the ferry icon to Old Mill, then chalks it on the board. The projection over the mist completes the same link in light.

Passengers nod. Ash smiles — counting is competence. Counting is identity.

*Audio direction: Ferry horn, low and long. Water slap against the hull. Distant choir, almost too faint to hear.*

CUT TO:

SC02
### EXT. PIER BENCH - DAY

**Time: Morning**

**Characters: ASH, WOMAN ON THE BENCH**

*Visual direction: Ticket-tap close-up with no boarding. Worn bench. Waiting posture held in fog. Thermos and knees.*

The ferry has returned to the pier. Beside the jetty, tucked against a bollard, is the bench by the pier — wood PEELed from decades of waiting. On it sits the WOMAN ON THE BENCH, still, hands folded, bag at her feet, watching the ferry as if counting returns.

She steps to the fare reader at the pier, not the ferry. CLOSE-UP: Her fare card taps. A soft chime.

She does not board.

The ramp closes. The ferry pulls away without her.

Ash, on the return run, has watched from behind the rail. Ash steps off onto the pier, replays the tap on the handheld log, and logs it. First anomaly.

ASH: That tap. No boarding.

Ash closes the notebook to the rule Mara wrote inside the cover: *Log, don't press.* Ash tries once anyway.

ASH: Are you waiting for someone?

The woman looks at the water, folds her hands tighter, and does not answer. She rises slowly — knees stiff, a small click audible — walks to the water fountain, fills a steel thermos, and returns to the bench by the pier to sit again. She does not leave the area; she returns to the same spot each time.

Ash logs the fare card that never boards: the tap with no boarding. Ash watches her pattern instead of asking again — same bench, same posture, same tap each cycle — and begins following her pattern.

*Audio direction: Fare reader chime. Ferry horn, distant. Mist muffling footsteps. Water slap.*

DISSOLVE TO:

SC03
### EXT. PIER BENCH - DAY

**Time: Midday**

**Characters: ASH, WOMAN ON THE BENCH, CAPTAIN MARA**

*Visual direction: Flowers, bench wear, and accumulated fare records. River mist time-lapse compressing the nine-hour cycle. Brief departures and returns between cycles.*

Midday light burns through the mist in a thin band, then fades again. Between cycles the woman briefly leaves the bench to stretch her legs, walks the pier length, flexes her knees, then returns to the bench by the pier. On the deck, Ash takes a layover break: Captain Mara shares soup from a thermos and a bread roll with Ash, Ash sits, rests knees, and returns for the next run.

Ash follows the remembered traces to the bench — the polished edge of the wood where hundreds of hands have rested, a small plastic-wrapped bunch of flowers tucked against the leg, a faint track in the gravel from the reader to this exact spot.

Ash runs the fare card history on the deck log. Page after page. Same card. Same pier. Same tap.

ASH: Same card. Every day.

ASH: Forty years. Ash uncovers the forty years of fares.

The log overlays the notebook map — forty years of light-points, all anchored to one bench. River mist time-lapse passes over the bench through the morning and into the afternoon while the woman remains seated, apart from her short departures and returns.

The woman has been sitting there long enough to be furniture.

The bench is revealed as the daily anchor, not a stop — the forty years of fares point to a person.

*Audio direction: Fare reader chime, repeated in quiet rhythm. Water slap. Kettle ticking in the pilot house. No music — let the repeats do the work.*

CUT TO:

SC04
### INT. FERRY PILOT HOUSE - DAY

**Time: Afternoon**

**Characters: ASH, CAPTAIN MARA**

*Visual direction: Gap in the notebook map. Records for forty years of fares, stacked by year. Fog thickening outside the windows.*

A narrow, warm pilot house. Paper logbooks, tide chart, kettle. Through the glass, the river is gone — only white fog.

Ash alone with the records, then Captain Mara beside, supervising. Ash compares the memorised map against the logs, page by page. One line does not match. The notebook map has a gap — a blank water-space where a landing should be. The chalk board has the same blank.

Ash runs a finger along forty years of fares for the one card. Then the incident column, decades old: a man died on this crossing. His daily ticket, never surrendered.

ASH: The missing landing isn't a place.
ASH: It's him.

Ash closes the logbook, hand shaking. The meaning lands: the fare is her late husband's daily ticket, kept alive since his death. The woman has not been paying for herself. She has been paying for him, every day, for forty years.

CAPTAIN MARA: You counted the trips, Ash. You didn't count the reason. That happens to apprentices. What you do next is the job.

ASH: If I count only landings, the map is wrong.

*Audio direction: Pages turning. Kettle ticking. Outside, the ferry horn sounds and is swallowed by fog.*

DISSOLVE TO:

SC05
### EXT. REDBANK FERRY DECK - DUSK

**Time: Dusk**

**Characters: ASH, WOMAN ON THE BENCH, CAPTAIN MARA**

*Visual direction: Gold ink added to the practical notebook map. Flowers scattered on the water. Empty bench against river mist. Ferry horn.*

Dusk. The mist glows amber at its edges. Ash returns to the pier bench, no longer following — listening. Captain Mara stays on deck, keeping the ramp supervised.

The woman is still there. For the first time, she speaks.

WOMAN: He rode this ferry every day. Forty years.
WOMAN: I just... kept his seat.

Ash kneels beside the bench. No punch. No log. Just the route notebook and a gold pen from the pilot house.

ASH: Then his landing belongs on the map.

CLOSE-UP: In the notebook, Ash adds a final point in gold ink — the husband's landing, long missing — and chalks the same point on the deck board. The projection over the water glows faintly with the completed route. The map is complete, not because every person is counted, but because *why* they travel is finally understood.

The woman stands. Slowly. Knees click; she steadies herself on the bench, picks up her flowers, steps to the fare reader — this time, she boards the ferry.

Ash holds the rail for her. Captain Mara sounds the horn, warm and final.

The woman stands at the rail, opens the plastic, and scatters the flowers onto the river. They float, a pale line behind the ferry. She steps off at the next landing — one stop only — and is gone into the mist.

FINAL SHOT: The bench by the pier, empty, against the river mist.

*Audio direction: Ferry horn, warm and final. Water slap. Distant choir, now audible, then fading.*

FADE TO BLACK.

**END**

## Production Plan

### Map Medium

The living map is a practical, filmable system: Ash's weatherproof A5 route notebook with a hand-inked river map, coloured landing pins, and pencil/gold-ink updates, mirrored on a small chalk landing board at the gate and in the pilot house. The light-line projection over mist is a secondary in-camera projection visualisation of the same notebook/chalk information — it never replaces the physical prop. Hero beats (SC01 line-drawing, SC04 blank gap, SC05 gold-ink addition) are all shot on the notebook and chalk board for continuity.

- **Supervision and safety:** Ash is 19, a supervised apprentice on a signed work permit under Captain Mara Ruiz, licensed captain and safety coverage. Ash wears high-vis vest over life vest, remains behind the rail while moving, and Captain Mara is physically present for morning safety check (SC01), break/meal share (SC03), records supervision (SC04), and ramp oversight (SC05).
- **Social friction:** Passenger Omar corrects his landing in SC01, forcing Ash to listen and redraw, revealing Omar's character and showing the map is not infallible.
- **Not-asking rule:** Captain Mara's rule — log, don't press fare-payers — is written in the notebook and spoken in SC01; Ash breaks it once with a direct question in SC02 and the woman declines to answer.
- **Endurance:** Between cycles the woman briefly departs to stretch, fill her thermos, and returns to the bench by the pier; Ash's endurance is preserved by resting knees and sharing soup and bread with Captain Mara in SC03.

## Scene Cards Applied

- **SC01 — EXT. REDBANK FERRY DECK - DAY / Dawn:** Pride, competence, and a sense of control. The living map of landings built from memory in the route notebook.
- **SC02 — EXT. PIER BENCH - DAY / Morning:** Curiosity and unease. The tap with no boarding logged as the first anomaly; the fare card that never boards.
- **SC03 — EXT. PIER BENCH - DAY / Midday:** Patient investigation giving way to sorrow. The forty years of fares anchor to the bench by the pier.
- **SC04 — INT. FERRY PILOT HOUSE - DAY / Afternoon:** Shame, grief, and the collapse of counting as identity. The fare is the late husband's daily ticket, kept alive since his death on this crossing.
- **SC05 — EXT. REDBANK FERRY DECK - DUSK / Dusk:** Honouring, release, and quiet remembrance. Gold ink completes the map; she rides one stop to scatter flowers.
