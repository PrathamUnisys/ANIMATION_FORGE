FADE IN:

SC01
**INT. BASEMENT FLAT - NIGHT**

*A cramped, tidy basement flat. A clock reads 2:58 a.m. LAILA (30s, home carer, still in scrubs) lies awake, eyes open, listening to the building breathe. Fridge hum. Pipe tick. Her phone alarm is set for 5:30.*

*At 3:00, the ceiling answers: thud… drag… thud. **The footsteps at 3 a.m.** begin — measured, heavy, unmistakable.*

*Laila sits up, breath held. Dust sifts from a hairline crack, falling in time with each step. She counts under her breath, a carer’s habit to stay calm.*

LAILA  
One… two… three… hallway. Four… stop.

*The steps pause above her bedroom, then resume — not random, but patterned. Laila presses her palm to the ceiling as if to feel a pulse. The walking continues, indifferent.*

LAILA (CONT’D, whisper)  
You’re not real. You’re tired.

*But the clock ticks to 3:07 and the steps keep their appointment. Laila pulls a pillow over her head. The sound moves through cloth, through bone, through certainty.*

CUT TO:

SC02
**INT. BASEMENT FLAT / STAIRWELL - NIGHT (LATER)**

*3:22 a.m. The footsteps trace **the slow circuit** overhead: window to door to chair to window again, the same loop. Laila, unable to bear it, pulls on a cardigan and climbs the stairwell.*

*She reaches the landing of **the empty flat above** — a door with a faded number, a keyhole dark. A neighbour, MRS. DANE, opens her own door a crack, wary.*

MRS. DANE  
Love, it’s the middle of the night.

LAILA  
Do you hear it? The walking. Every night.

MRS. DANE  
That flat’s been empty nine years. Since the old tenant… well. No one’s been up there. Go back to bed.

*Mrs. Dane closes her door. Laila stands alone. Above her — silence now, sudden and complete, as if the walker is listening back. She tries the handle of **the empty flat above**: locked. Her own spare keys jangle in her pocket, useless here.*

*She retreats downstairs, heart hammering. At 3:40, the moment she lies down, **the footsteps at 3 a.m.** resume exactly where they left off.*

CUT TO:

SC03
**INT. BASEMENT FLAT - NIGHT (NEXT NIGHT)**

*Laila sets traps for truth: her phone propped to record audio, a line of salt at her door, her shoes placed neatly. 2:59 a.m. She watches the second hand.*

*3:00. Thud. Drag. **The slow circuit** begins again. The salt line remains unbroken. The door stays shut. The sound is only above.*

*On playback in the morning (grey light through curtains), the recording hisses — then, faintly, the footsteps are there. Laila stares, vindicated and terrified.*

*That evening, exhausted from a shift, she finds the letting agent’s master key in her bag — borrowed, forgotten. She hesitates, then climbs the stairs at 2:50 a.m., determined to stand in **the empty flat above** when 3:00 comes.*

CUT TO:

SC04
**INT. EMPTY FLAT ABOVE - NIGHT**

*Laila unlocks the door and steps into **the empty flat above**. Moonlight shows bare rooms, dust, silence. No furniture — except, in the centre of the living room, **the one chair**, placed facing the window as if someone just stood up.*

*Laila’s breath catches. On a shelf by the door sit **the keys on the shelf** — her keys, her fob, the one with the chipped star. She pats her pocket: empty.*

LAILA  
No. I had those.

*She moves through the flat, each room a mirror of her own life arranged wrong. In the bedroom, on a dresser, stands her framed photo — but it is **the photograph turned to the wall**, face hidden.*

*Laila turns it over with shaking hands: it’s her and her late mother, smiling. She remembers turning this photo down weeks ago because she couldn’t bear to look. Her knees weaken.*

LAILA (CONT’D)  
I did this.

*The clock on her phone flips to 3:00. From below — from her own bedroom — a faint thud. Laila runs downstairs.*

CUT TO:

SC05
**INT. BASEMENT FLAT / EMPTY FLAT ABOVE - DAWN**

*Laila bursts into her basement flat: her bed is rumpled in a shape that suggests she’s been lying there, but her shoes by the door are dusted with plaster from upstairs. She checks her phone camera roll — and finds photos she doesn’t remember taking: **the one chair**, **the slow circuit** traced in dust, **the photograph turned to the wall**. Sleepwalking. Grief walking.*

*Flash-montage (quick, silent): Laila rising at night, unlocking **the empty flat above**, placing **the keys on the shelf**, turning the photo away, pacing **the slow circuit**, then returning to bed — the tenant above was her all along.*

*Dawn light. Laila sits on her bed, shaking but clear. She takes one deliberate photograph of herself, awake, facing the camera. Then she climbs upstairs once more, gently turns **the photograph turned to the wall** back to face the room, and leaves **the one chair** by the window.*

*She locks the door, places **the keys on the shelf** inside for the morning, and keeps her own copy in her hand. Back in the basement, she sets her alarm, lies down, and closes her eyes.*

*At 3:00 a.m. the ceiling is silent. For the first time, the building holds only her breathing. Laila sleeps — not cured, but honest with the dark.*

FADE OUT.
