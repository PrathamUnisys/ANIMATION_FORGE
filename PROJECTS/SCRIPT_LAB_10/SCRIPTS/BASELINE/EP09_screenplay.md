FADE IN:

SC01
**EXT. KESTREL HOLLOW - DAWN**

*A withered valley: cracked earth, dry troughs, orchards bare. No birdsong — only wind. Above the square hang **the six seasonal bells** in an open frame, green with age, mute. PIP (13, apprentice, ear cocked as if listening to metal) runs into the square with ELDER MAUD (60s, stern, worried).*

PIP  
Listen. The iron is humming under the hush. It wants to be struck.

ELDER MAUD  
Wanting is not law, Pip. We ring spring, summer, harvest, frost, seed, and storm. Six. Never more.

PIP  
But the blight came when the seventh went missing. Look — **the birds silent over Kestrel Hollow**, the wells coughing dust. The old songs say the hollow needs all seven.

*Pip points beyond the square to a mound of rubble where a tower once stood. Half-buried in nettles lies a great bell on its side — **the seventh bell with no tower**, cold and waiting.*

ELDER MAUD  
That bell broke its tower a hundred years ago. We leave it be.

PIP  
Then I’ll find how to ring it without a tower. I can hear **the iron that sings** — it’s calling from somewhere deeper.

CUT TO:

SC02
**EXT. KESTREL HOLLOW WELL - DAY**

*Pip and Maud approach the village well, its rope frayed, bucket dry. Pip leans over the rim; a faint, pure tone vibrates up through the stone.*

PIP  
There. Do you hear it?

ELDER MAUD  
I hear thirst.

*Pip lowers a lantern on a cord. The light reveals **the well's throat of stone** narrowing below — and wedged across it, catching the last trickle of water, is **the clapper in the well**, a heavy iron tongue snagged like a heart in a ribcage.*

PIP  
The seventh bell’s clapper. It fell and lodged in **the well's throat of stone**. That’s why the bell is mute — and why the water chokes.

ELDER MAUD  
If you pull it free, the throat may collapse. The well is all we have left.

PIP  
If we leave it, the hollow dies of thirst anyway. There must be a way to ring without breaking.

CUT TO:

SC03
**EXT. BELL FRAME / HOLLOW - DAY**

*Pip tests ideas beneath **the six seasonal bells**, tapping them gently. Each answers with a season’s tone. Villagers gather, murmuring.*

PIP  
Six voices, six promises. The seventh is the promise of return — water, wings, waking. **The iron that sings** doesn’t need a tower if it has a throat to answer it.

ELDER MAUD  
The law says do not raise what fell.

PIP  
The law was written when we had rain. Laws can learn, Elder.

*Pip holds a rope, measuring the distance from the bell to the well. An idea forms — not to pull the clapper out, but to make it speak where it is.*

PIP (CONT’D)  
I won’t pull **the clapper in the well**. I’ll ring it.

ELDER MAUD  
From above? With no tower?

PIP  
With a rope, a song, and the stone itself.

CUT TO:

SC04
**EXT. KESTREL HOLLOW WELL - DUSK**

*Golden dusk. Pip rigs a long rope down **the well's throat of stone**, looped around **the clapper in the well** without dislodging it. Villagers hold the well’s rim steady. Maud watches, fear and hope in her eyes.*

ELDER MAUD  
If the stone shifts—

PIP  
Then I’ll sing softer. Trust me to listen.

*Pip closes their eyes, ear to the rope, hearing **the iron that sings**. Instead of yanking, Pip draws the rope in a slow, resonant rhythm — bowing the clapper against the stone throat like a bow on a string.*

*A low note rises, strange and beautiful, vibrating through rock and water. The well hums. Ripples shiver in the trapped water below.*

PIP (CONT’D, singing the tone)  
Come back. Come back.

*The note deepens. Dust falls — but the stones hold. The clapper rings in place, and the well answers: a surge of water bubbles up, clear and strong, overflowing the rim.*

CUT TO:

SC05
**EXT. KESTREL HOLLOW - DAWN (NEXT MORNING)**

*Water runs in the troughs. The square is wet and shining. Pip stands by **the seventh bell with no tower**, rope still tied, smiling with exhausted pride. Maud places a hand on Pip’s shoulder.*

ELDER MAUD  
You rang what we buried. And the hollow drank.

*A sound returns — faint at first, then unmistakable: wings. A single bird calls, then another. Flocks wheel in over the valley, breaking the long silence.*

*With the seventh toll still echoing from the well, **the birds silent over Kestrel Hollow** are silent no more. Song fills the air, answering **the six seasonal bells** as Pip gently taps each in turn, the seventh humming below like a heartbeat.*

PIP  
Seven, at last. Not a tower — a promise kept.

ELDER MAUD  
The law will remember this as courage.

*Pip laughs, wiping muddy hands. The hollow, watered and singing, wakes to a new season. The camera rises over bells, birds, and bright water.*

FADE OUT.
