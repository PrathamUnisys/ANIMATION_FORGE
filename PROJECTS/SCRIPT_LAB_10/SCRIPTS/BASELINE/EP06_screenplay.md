FADE IN:

SC01
**INT. ALL SAINTS CONFESSION BOOTH - NIGHT**

*Rain lashes stained glass. The church is dark except for Wren’s lantern as she mops the nave. She pauses at the confession booth — door ajar, cold air spilling out like breath.*

WREN pushes the door wider. On the lattice and wood bloom impossible fern-shapes of ice: **the frost patterns in the booth**, delicate and wrong in a room with no winter.

WREN  
*(to herself, instinct old as a badge)*  
Don’t touch. Don’t breathe on it.

*Inside, slumped on the penitent’s bench, is a MAN (40s) — eyes open, skin waxy, no wound. His fingers clutch a folded paper. FATHER OWEN (50s) hurries in, cassock damp.*

FATHER OWEN  
Wren? I heard— God preserve us.

WREN  
He’s gone. And this isn’t weather.

*Owen reaches to close the man’s eyes; Wren catches his wrist.*

WREN (CONT’D)  
One night, Father. Before the parish buries this as a bad heart. I’ve seen marks like these in a case that cost me my job. Give me till morning.

FATHER OWEN  
Mrs. Keel wants him moved before dawn mass. If this is… unnatural—

WREN  
Then moving him hides it. One night.

*Owen looks at **the frost patterns in the booth**, fear and duty wrestling. He nods once.*

FATHER OWEN  
Till the first bell. No longer.

CUT TO:

SC02
**INT. ALL SAINTS CONFESSION BOOTH - NIGHT (CONTINUOUS)**

*Wren works by lantern: photographing, sketching, not disturbing. She notices the priest’s side lattice — a narrow wooden seam.*

WREN  
This booth was repaired last month?

FATHER OWEN  
After the storm. New hinge, new panel.

*Wren tests it. With a careful push, **the sliding panel of the booth** shifts a finger’s width, exhaling a deeper cold. Frost feathers the groove.*

WREN  
A hiding place that breathes. Someone used this.

*She spots crumbs on the kneeler — a broken host. She lifts a fragment with tweezers: **the communion wafer's chill** bites through the metal, colder than ice should be.*

WREN (CONT’D)  
Father… this wafer’s been somewhere no bread belongs.

FATHER OWEN *(shaken)*  
The tabernacle was locked. I checked it at vespers.

WREN  
Then the cold came to the wafer, not the other way around.

*She unfolds the paper in the dead man’s hand — cramped writing: **the man's confession note**.*

WREN (reading)  
“I took what was not mine. If found, do not open the panel. The rope will finish it.” Finish what?

*A distant metallic groan echoes from the belfry. Both freeze.*

CUT TO:

SC03
**INT. ALL SAINTS SACRISTY - NIGHT**

*Wren and Owen move through shadowed aisles to the sacristy. The poor-box sits open, empty. Frost traces the lock.*

WREN  
He confessed to theft. “Took what was not mine.” Poor-box.

FATHER OWEN  
He was our handyman. Thomas Reed. He had keys, access… a temper, but not this.

*Wren lays out clues on a cloth: sketch of **the frost patterns in the booth**, the chilled fragment, the note.*

WREN  
He built a cold trap and warned someone not to open it. Traps are for thieves — or for guilty men who expect to be followed.

FATHER OWEN  
You think he meant to harm another?

WREN  
I think he meant to protect his secret. And secrets in this church have a rope.

*From above, a single bell note rings — clear, unmanned. Owen crosses himself.*

FATHER OWEN  
The bell… no one is there.

WREN  
Then take me to it.

CUT TO:

SC04
**INT. ALL SAINTS BELFRY / BOOTH ACCESS - NIGHT**

*They climb narrow stairs. In the belfry, **the bell rope** hangs still — yet frost glazes its fibers near a hidden pulley feed running down a shaft toward the booth wall.*

WREN  
Not a ghost. A line.

*She traces the mechanism: the rope rigged through the shaft to a compartment behind **the sliding panel of the booth**. Inside the compartment (viewed through the opened panel) sits a cracked chemical ice-box — salts and tin, old handyman’s kit — its venting tube aimed into the booth.*

WREN (CONT’D)  
He packed a freezing mix, ran **the bell rope** as a trigger to vent it when the panel was forced. A burglar’s snare for anyone who found the poor-box money he hid.

FATHER OWEN  
Then why is he the one dead inside?

*Wren re-reads **the man's confession note** under lantern light, aligning times and smudges.*

WREN  
Because he tested it alone. See the smudge — he pulled, the panel jammed, the vent opened into the booth instead of out. **The bell rope that tolled itself** wasn’t a spirit, Father. It was the mechanism slipping under his weight as he tried to stop it.

*Owen sinks onto a step, grief replacing fear.*

FATHER OWEN  
He came to confess… and sat inside his own trap.

WREN  
A confession can be its own punishment. The cold killed him before he could finish making it right.

CUT TO:

SC05
**INT. ALL SAINTS CONFESSION BOOTH - DAWN**

*Pale dawn through stained glass. MRS. KEEL arrives, stern, but Wren presents the evidence calmly: the rig, the note, the chilled wafer placed near the vent.*

WREN  
No haunting. A hidden ice-box, a rigged rope, and a man who died by the snare he built. You can bury him with the truth.

MRS. KEEL  
The parish will not be a spectacle.

FATHER OWEN  
It will be honest. That is enough.

*Keel relents. The booth is roped off as evidence, not rumor. Owen carefully places **the communion wafer's chill** fragment in a reliquary envelope — a record, not a relic.*

*Wren gathers her lantern. Dawn light warms the thawing edges of **the frost patterns in the booth**.*

FATHER OWEN (CONT’D)  
Will you stay for mass, Wren?

WREN  
I’ll stay to finish the floor. Some things you clean by telling the truth.

*She looks once at the silent rope above — still now, ordinary. A final, quiet bell tone is heard in her memory only, then fades. Wren exhales: a case closed, a small redemption earned.*

FADE OUT.
