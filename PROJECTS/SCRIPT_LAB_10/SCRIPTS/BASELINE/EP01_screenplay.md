FADE IN:

SC01
**EXT. BELMONT COTTAGE CHIMNEY STACK - PRE-DAWN**

*Grey fog hugs a foggy Edwardian village lane. The first hint of dawn pales the eastern sky, but the cottage is still in shadow. A village bell tolls once, far off. A stray music-hall piano drifts from somewhere unseen.*

CLARA BELMONT (60s, shawl, lantern low) steps into her yard with a practiced morning routine — latch, woodpile, a glance at a framed photograph of her late sister’s family on the sill. She stops.

ON THE CHIMNEY STACK: fresh chalk marks — **the dancing stick-figures on the chimney**, one bowed, one leapt, one with arms to the sky. Her lantern light catches the sharp white scratch in mortar and brick.

*Close on Clara’s fingertip tracing a figure. Chalk dust lifts. She wipes one mark: still fresh. She breathes out, a small cloud in the cold pre-dawn air.*

CLARA  
*(whisper, to herself)*  
Fresh. And meant for me.

*She looks down the empty lane. No footsteps. No witness. Protective unease settles into curiosity. She copies the postures carefully in her mind, then turns back inside, choosing silence for now.*

CUT TO:

SC02
**INT. BELMONT COTTAGE KITCHEN - NIGHT**

*Warm lamplight, deep shadow. Rain taps the window. A kettle ticks on the hob. Clara sits at a scrubbed table with slate, chalk, and an old school reader. THOMAS PIKE (20s, soaked coat, notebook) bursts in, breathless.*

THOMAS  
Mrs. Pike at the post office swears you’ve had dancers visiting in the dark. Is it true?

CLARA  
Mrs. Pike should mend her roof before she mends other people’s lives.

*Clara slides a slate across the table: one copied dancing figure.*

THOMAS  
The chimney?

CLARA  
I copied it at dawn. I won’t have the village dancing around my hearth for sport.

THOMAS  
If it’s a code, it’s a story. If it’s a warning, it’s twice the story.

CLARA  
It is neither until we know.

*Thomas opens his notebook to a headline: SCANDAL AT BELMONT? Clara closes it gently, firmly.*

CLARA (CONT’D)  
No headline. A teacher’s logic first. Posture, then step, then turn.

*She opens the school reader to a rhyme exercise and reads, slow: “Pick a posy, turn a toe, skip a step where chimneys glow—” Thomas’s eyes widen.*

THOMAS  
That’s the street rhyme. Clara — that’s **the nursery rhyme anagram clue**. Posy, toe, step… the figures aren’t dancing. They’re holding letters.

*Split-screen feel in action: chalk posture beside Thomas’s notebook; a leg becomes an L, a bow becomes a C.*

CLARA  
Count by the rhyme. One for posy, two for toe. Read it again — carefully.

THOMAS  
“Come home.” “Beware.” Shift again… “Come at dawn.” It’s **the broken anagram cipher-nursery rhyme** — broken on purpose, mended by the count.

*Thomas scribbles, then pauses at an empty space.*

THOMAS (CONT’D)  
One more line and we could print. They say a betrothal will be named before church.

CLARA  
Then we decode before we breathe. No printing. Decoding.

THOMAS  
You’d keep a secret that could ruin two families?

CLARA  
I’d keep a truth private until it can be told decently. That is not the same thing.

*An alliance forms in the quiet. Thomas closes the notebook.*

CUT TO:

SC03
**INT. BELMONT COTTAGE KITCHEN - NIGHT (LATER)**

*The kettle has gone quiet. Shadows press past the window — village silhouettes, umbrellas, whispers. A loud, authoritative knock.*

*AUNT HARROW (50s, dark dress, iron posture) enters without removing her hat, clutching a sealed envelope like a verdict. She places it on the table: **the aunt's betrothal letter**.*

AUNT HARROW  
Mrs. Belmont. By family order, delivered before witnesses. The betrothal is announced in the square at dawn. A public notice. No more chalk, no more code, no more scandal.

CLARA  
A private letter is not best delivered as a public threat, Madam.

AUNT HARROW  
Privacy is a luxury this family can no longer afford. Reputation is kept by announcement.

*She exits. The door slams. Silhouettes scatter, but the gossip lingers like smoke. Close on the broken seal: ANNUAL BETROTHAL — TO BE PROCLAIMED AT DAWN.*

THOMAS  
We have minutes, not hours.

CLARA  
Then we read faster.

*They return to slate and figures. Chalk dust on their fingertips.*

THOMAS  
“Come at dawn, coward—” no. That can’t be the end of it.

CLARA  
No threat ever began with a confession. Read the author, Thomas, not the accusation.

*Thomas studies a tally-like posture, then points.*

THOMAS  
The sweep’s mark. Every chimney sweep keeps a tally. Clara — the ledger.

*Clara pulls a battered book from the shelf: **the chimney sweep's ledger** (BELMONT PARISH). She flips pages; close on cramped handwriting and a mark beside a name dated three winters past.*

CLARA  
Handwriting and sequence. Not a stranger. He worked the chimney to be seen without being seen. **The confession in the chimney sweep's ledger** is his — I can feel it in the hand.

THOMAS  
Then the announcement is the trap. Shame him at dawn before anyone reads the whole.

CUT TO:

SC04
**INT. BELMONT COTTAGE KITCHEN - NIGHT INTO DAWN**

*Blue-black night thins. Cold teacups replace the kettle. Ledger pages flip in frantic quiet. Clara stands at the slate, exhausted but bright-eyed. Thomas writes. Aunt Harrow returns to the doorway, pulled back by Thomas’s urgent message, gossip still clinging to her.*

AUNT HARROW  
What have you learned that warrants dragging me from my bed for a child’s doodle?

CLARA  
That I was tempted to scrub the chimney clean and say nothing. That Thomas was tempted to print it. And you were ready to announce it.

THOMAS *(reading the completed message, slow and clear)*  
“Come at dawn, coward heir. I left in haste. I fled from duty, not from wrong. Forgive the house. Forgive the name.” He calls himself coward — not you.

*Dawn light edges the window. Ledger entries align with the final postures.*

CLARA  
Read it whole. **The confession in the chimney sweep's ledger** begs forgiveness, not punishment. Public shaming would destroy him for daring to be sorry.

AUNT HARROW  
The family will not survive a runaway heir confessing by chalk.

CLARA  
The family will not survive betraying a private confession to keep a public façade. Choose which name you mean to keep.

*Clara holds out the opened letter. Her hand is steady. A long beat. Aunt Harrow takes it, crumpled now in her glove, resistance breaking.*

AUNT HARROW  
Withdrawn. I will go to the square myself. No name will be ruined there today.

*She exits. Dawn pours through the kitchen window, clear and cold.*

CUT TO:

SC05
**EXT. BELMONT COTTAGE CHIMNEY STACK - DAWN**

*Fog lifts. Sun touches the highest bricks. Clara and Thomas stand below the chimney, looking up. The village is quiet; silhouettes gone. A bell tolls once, resolved. Thomas writes a single line in his notebook, then closes it.*

THOMAS (V.O.)  
Belmont household safe after night of public rumour. Family matter resolved privately.

*Aunt Harrow walks down the lane, spine straight, letter pocketed. Then — a faint chalk scratch.*

*On the chimney a new figure appears: a single stick-figure in a deep bow, arms swept low in thanks.*

*Clara and Thomas watch in silent profile as the music-hall piano returns, soft and content.*

CLARA  
He is still in the village, then.

THOMAS  
Still in the village. And for once, that’s enough.

*They hold the moment. The bowing figure catches the morning light.*

FADE OUT.
