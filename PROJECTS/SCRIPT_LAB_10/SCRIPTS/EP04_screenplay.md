# THE CARTOGRAPHER OF SMALL YEARS — v2

*Anime-inspired slow-burn drama. Approx. 10 minutes, 600 seconds. Original characters and world.*

**Premise:** A long-lived mapmaker who has outlived five villages maps a single day for a short-lived apprentice, learning what a day is for.

**Story Question:** What is a day worth to someone who has lived two hundred years of them?

**FINAL PAGE LOCKED:** The final line is MASTER ILDAN: Now I am watching. The final action is Ildan standing still, head tilted up beside Tern. The closing image is two small figures under the meteor shower on the hills with no easel, no atlas, and no pen.

**EFFECT RULE — INK BLOOM:** The ink bloom is memory visualization, not literal magic. It appears only in SC01 flashback inserts as a flat 2D line animation in gold, blue, and green, composited over the desaturated panorama, growing outward from Ildan's pen point at center frame for three to four seconds per insert, then fading. The camera holds the same wide framing and focus stays on the easel. No character sees or touches the bloom, and it never appears again. All maps in SC02 through SC04 are diegetic paper only. Total bloom time is under twenty seconds.

**SOUND TREATMENT:** Sound is drawn only on paper. In SC03, the distant bell is rendered in close-up as three thin concentric arcs inked onto the one-day sheet, intercut with a cutaway reaction shot of Tern listening with his head tilted. No arcs appear in the air or the sky. This keeps all line work paper-anchored after SC01, consistent with the effect rule.

FADE IN:

SC01
### EXT. VALLEY OVERLOOK - DAWN

**VISUAL DIRECTION:** Wide, still panorama. Mist in the valley. Sunrise light touches field boundaries. The camera is static, then slowly pushes in.

**AUDIO DIRECTION:** Dawn birds, distant wind. The recurring pen scratch is close-miked, rhythmic, almost metronomic.

A wooden easel. An immense open atlas — the valley atlas of two centuries — fills the easel. Hundreds of hand-ruled boundaries in faded ink. The paper is yellowed, repaired, impossibly thick.

MASTER ILDAN enters frame from behind the easel, already drawing. He is late sixties in appearance, slender, ink-stained fingers, a long coat, a cap he never takes off. He does not look up. His pen moves, scratches, stops to listen.

His hand completes a boundary. Where his ink touches the paper, memory visualization blooms: faint luminous ink-maps unfold across the desaturated valley. Thin hedgerows bloom gold, river courses bloom blue, fields bloom green, growing outward from his pen stroke like time-lapse. The bloom stays flat, centered, and brief. Ildan does not react to it. Focus holds on the easel.

**AUDIO DIRECTION:** Pen scratch continues under a soft, low string swell.

MASTER ILDAN (V.O.): Hollowmere. When the alder stood at the north corner. Barrowsfield. When the daughters planted the barley. Lark's End. When the old miller still drew water. Oakhaven. When the gate was iron. Mirelow. When the reeds were cut in winter.

He names the field boundaries of five villages in a quiet, practiced litany, two hundred years old, without looking up.

*FLASHBACKS — SLOW, FRAGMENTED, DESATURATED. Three to four seconds each, over the same wide framing. No titles.*

A market square that no longer exists. A family laughing at a harvest table. A child running a hoop down a boundary path. A wedding dance in a field. All gone. Ildan watches each memory the way he watches a boundary: to record it, not to enter it.

The ink-maps bloom again inside the memory inserts, faster. He is adding another layer. Another decade. The pen does not hesitate.

MASTER ILDAN (V.O.): Two hundred years, and I have named them all. Every family. Every field. The boundaries hold. The people do not.

He draws. The ink blooms. He remains outside the bloom, behind the easel.

LONG PAUSE. The scratch fades as birds call.

CUT TO:

SC02
### INT. MAPMAKER'S WORKSHOP - NIGHT

**VISUAL DIRECTION:** Interior, crowded and warm. Lantern light flickers. Hundreds of rolled maps. The immense atlas is chained open on a central table. The camera tracks low over paper and hands.

**AUDIO DIRECTION:** Night crickets outside, lantern crackle. Pen scratch returns, then stops.

MASTER ILDAN enters, sets down his pen, turns a page with excessive care. He studies it as if for flaws.

TERN enters through the workshop door, a teen apprentice with a small satchel, an empty sheet of paper, and a small tonic vial tucked in his satchel flap. He steps in, breathes shallow for a beat, presses one hand briefly to the stairs rail to steady himself, then recovers. He unscrews the vial, takes one measured morning-tonic dose, and swallows. His fingers tremble slightly. A folded physician's note in his satchel reads "one dose — rest." Ildan's eyes rest on the vial. He does not ask for the physician's words; the question stays stored, unasked.

TERN: Master. Am I late?

MASTER ILDAN: You are never late. You are early to a life that is already short.

Ildan does not smile. Tern steps forward, reverent. His hands hover over the atlas.

Ahead lies the map Tern touches first: a small, newer sheet, not the atlas. A simple sketch of a path and a tree. His fingertip lingers on the tree's leaves.

TERN: This one. You drew this last spring.

Ildan follows his gaze, surprised, then impatient.

MASTER ILDAN: That was a study. A rehearsal. It is incomplete. The branches do not correspond to the actual spread.

TERN: They do. I sat under it. That is how the light fell.

Tern studies the immense atlas — the valley atlas of two centuries — running his eyes across five villages of field boundaries, page after page. A quiet bond forms. He looks at Ildan, not the map, for the first time.

TERN: What did all of it mean to you?

MASTER ILDAN: It meant nothing is lost.

Tern lays the empty sheet down for the apprentice's one-day map. He touches its edge carefully.

TERN: Then will you map one day for me? Not another decade. One perfect day. Today. As it is lived.

Beat.

Ildan takes the empty sheet, lays it carefully aside, unused.

MASTER ILDAN: No. A day is too small to matter. A decade is honest. A lifetime is honest. A day is a fragment that wastes the life around it.

TERN: I asked once at harvest. I am not asking again.

Silence. Tern's breathing is audible, shallow. Ildan looks at the sheet, then at the vial in Tern's satchel, then back at the sheet.

He picks the empty sheet up again. His hand is still.

MASTER ILDAN: One day. Today. I will draw it.

TERN: Today?

MASTER ILDAN: At dawn. Take the sheet. We start.

Ildan approves the one-day map explicitly. He does not explain. He holds the sheet out.

DISSOLVE TO:

SC03
### EXT. VALLEY OVERLOOK - DAY

**VISUAL DIRECTION:** Brighter, harsher daylight than dawn. The same easel and atlas, but now a single large day-sheet. Tern stands beside Ildan, hands clasped, not drawing — witnessing. The camera alternates between Ildan's fast hand and Tern's slow hands by his sides, then touching things. All line work stays on paper.

**AUDIO DIRECTION:** Wind in wheat, one distant bell. Pen scratch at double speed.

MASTER ILDAN enters already drawing fast and exhaustively. He draws only surfaces: the geometry of fields, the exact angle of a roof, the number of stones in a wall.

TERN enters after him, walking the overlook, touching a post, brushing wheat with his palm.

MASTER ILDAN: The horizon is bent here. Do you see? The contour must be true. If the contour is wrong, the valley is wrong, and everyone after us is wrong.

TERN: Listen.

He stops. Wind moves through the wheat below. A distant bell tolls once from the village below.

**AUDIO DIRECTION:** Wind in wheat swells. One distant bell tone, long decay.

Ildan's pen stops mid-line. He looks at Tern's hands, not his own.

TERN: That.

Ildan erases his exhaustive lines — not completely, but he stops drawing surfaces. He begins again, slower. His lines are imperfect, personal. He draws what Tern touches, hears, and misses: hands over paper, wheat bending, and the bell.

CLOSE-UP ON THE SHEET: Ildan inks three thin concentric arcs for the bell's sound, a sound-wave overlay on the paper surface only. CUTAWAY: Tern's head tilts, eyes closed, listening. His hands are still. The arcs do not leave the paper. They do not appear in the sky.

MASTER ILDAN: I do not know how to draw a sound.

TERN: You draw the part that hears it.

Ildan adjusts. His lines waver. He leaves room between them. Wind in wheat. Tern's stillness guides his attention.

TERN: Master. What did your years mean to you?

Beat. The pen has stopped.

MASTER ILDAN: They meant... I remember every boundary. I remember every name. I remember burying families and then recording where their fields would be divided next spring. My memory is a burden of outliving them.

TERN: The physician says I may not live another decade. I will not outlive a map. That is why I asked for one day.

Silence. Wheat moves. Ildan looks at his own ink-stained hands for the first time in the film.

CUT TO:

SC04
### INT. MAPMAKER'S WORKSHOP - NIGHT

**VISUAL DIRECTION:** The same workshop, now intimate. One lantern close to the table. The immense atlas is open to its final blank page at the back. The small one-day map lies beside it. The camera stays close on hands and ink. All maps are diegetic paper.

**AUDIO DIRECTION:** Lantern crackle only. Pen scratch returns, slower, hesitating.

MASTER ILDAN sits. TERN sits opposite, leaning in.

Ildan draws by lantern-light. He abandons perfection. His line wavers. He leaves blank space within the detailed lines — a large, intentional emptiness where Tern's head was when he was listening, where the wheat was bending.

MASTER ILDAN: I do not know what goes in the empty part.

TERN: The living. What is not yet finished.

Ildan nods. Thread, needle, careful hands. He binds the small, brief one-day map — the lantern-lit page — into the atlas as its final page, instead of another decade. The small page is not the grandest, but it is finished.

He presses his palm to it.

MASTER ILDAN: It is beautiful.

TERN: You have never said that before.

MASTER ILDAN: Teach me again. How to leave space.

TERN: You do not draw the wind. You leave room for it to move.

Ildan looks at the blank space. He does not fill it.

**AUDIO DIRECTION:** Pen scratch stops. Silence, then crickets.

DISSOLVE TO:

SC05
### EXT. HILLS - NIGHT

**VISUAL DIRECTION:** Wide night. Unlit hills, larger than the valley overlook. Stars, then meteors. Two small figures in the lower third of frame. No easel. No atlas. No pen. The camera is static, then slowly tilts up.

**AUDIO DIRECTION:** Night crickets, soft wind. No pen scratch. Silence replaces the recurring sound.

MASTER ILDAN enters running, then stops running. TERN enters with him, catching breath, then also stops.

Ildan has only ever charted this hill. He knows every meteor path on paper. He has never looked up from the chart. His head tilts up now, for the first time. Tern stands at his shoulder.

A meteor streaks. Then another. The meteor shower on the hills unfolds — silent, brief, bright.

TERN: It is starting.

MASTER ILDAN: I know every path. I never looked up.

TERN: Now?

MASTER ILDAN: Now I am watching.

FINAL IMAGE: Two small figures stand still under the sky, faces lit by a bright meteor trail crossing the hills. Ildan's hand hangs open at his side. Tern's shoulder rests against his arm. No map covers the sky.

FADE OUT.
