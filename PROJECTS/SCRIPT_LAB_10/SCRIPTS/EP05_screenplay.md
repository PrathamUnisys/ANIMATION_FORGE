FADE IN:

SC01
**EXT. LOTUS RIVER TIME - DAY**

*A wide, sunlit Indian river thick with lotus leaves and pink blooms. Dragonflies skim the water. Temple bells murmur from a far bank. Monsoon frogs chorus softly.*

JAMBUG the monkey swings along a mango-laden branch overhanging the river, laughing. Below, MAGAR the crocodile glides through lilies, eyes bright and kind.

This is **the lotus river friendship**: easy, daily, wordless at first.

JAMBUG  
Magar! The yellow ones fell at dawn. Sweet as last week’s, I swear.

MAGAR  
And the river kept the best shade for you, Jambug. Toss one before the current steals my patience.

*Jambug drops a mango; Magar catches it gently in his jaws and places it on a flat stone to share. Close on **the mangoes traded daily** — fruit passed paw to snout, a ritual of trust.*

JAMBUG  
One for you, one for me, one for the story you owe me.

MAGAR  
The story is the same: the river gives, and we do not take more than we can carry.

JAMBUG  
A good story. Boring. But good.

*They laugh — Jambug chattering, Magar’s tail thumping the water in slow delight. The friendship is the river’s rhythm: generous, unafraid.*

*A shadow crosses the far bank: THE RIVERBANK WOMAN hurries along with an empty pot, worry etched in her face. Jambug watches, curious, as she pauses to listen to a VOICE hawking remedies.*

FALSE ASTROLOGER (O.S.)  
Misfortune clings to silted homes! I read the stars — I sell relief!

*Jambug frowns. Magar follows his gaze, uneasy.*

MAGAR  
Trouble walks on two legs today.

JAMBUG  
Then let it keep walking.

*But the woman’s shoulders sag as she counts coins in her palm. The camera holds on **the mangoes traded daily** between friends — and on the worry crossing the water toward them.*

DISSOLVE TO:

SC02
**EXT. RIVERBANK VILLAGE - DUSK**

*Cooking smoke, damp earth, a small house with a flooded threshold. The RIVERBANK WOMAN sits with her husband’s tools, eyes red. The FALSE ASTROLOGER (a voice behind a bead curtain / implied presence) counts coins.*

FALSE ASTROLOGER (O.S.)  
Your fields drown, your stores rot — the stars are clear. Only **the astrologer's false cure** can turn your luck: a charm made from a monkey’s heart, fresh and willing.

RIVERBANK WOMAN  
A… heart?

FALSE ASTROLOGER (O.S.)  
Bring me **the astrologer's fee** by morning, and bring the heart. Or keep your misfortune. Choose.

*Close on her trembling hand placing coins — **the astrologer's fee** — into a bowl. Shame and desperation war in her face.*

*Cut to the river’s edge. Magar surfaces as the woman approaches, clutching the bowl.*

RIVERBANK WOMAN  
Magar. You know the monkey on the mango tree. The astrologer says only a monkey’s heart will save us.

MAGAR *(stung)*  
Jambug is my friend.

RIVERBANK WOMAN  
And my children are hungry. Please. I am asking you for **the heart demanded as price**.

*The words hang heavy. Magar sinks lower in the water, torn — loyalty pulling one way, the household that shelters him pulling the other.*

MAGAR  
I… will speak to him.

*The woman nods, relief and guilt mixing. Night insects rise.*

CUT TO:

SC03
**EXT. LOTUS RIVER TIME - DAY (NEXT MORNING)**

*Bright water, lotus blooms open. Jambug waits on his branch with two mangoes, puzzled by Magar’s slow approach.*

JAMBUG  
You’re late. The river kept asking for you.

MAGAR  
Jambug… my home is in sorrow. They say a cure needs— *(he cannot finish)* —they say it needs your heart.

*Silence. A dragonfly hovers. Jambug studies Magar’s face — not cunning, but cornered.*

JAMBUG  
My heart. Here, beating, trading mangoes with you. That heart?

MAGAR  
I promised to bring you across. I did not promise to harm you. But **the heart demanded as price** is what they ask.

JAMBUG *(softly)*  
And what do you ask, friend?

*Magar’s eyes glisten. He cannot answer. Jambug breathes, thinking — nīti, the old wit of survival without cruelty.*

JAMBUG (CONT’D)  
Then take me. But you know we monkeys keep our hearts safe when we travel — dry, hidden, so the river cannot claim them.

MAGAR  
Hidden… where?

JAMBUG  
In **the den under the lotus roots**, of course. I left mine there this morning, foolishly. Take me back and I’ll fetch it for you. A heart given is better than a heart taken.

*Magar, desperate to believe there is a path without betrayal, nods.*

MAGAR  
Show me.

*Jambug climbs onto Magar’s back. They glide toward the far bank — trust stretched thin as a reed.*

CUT TO:

SC04
**EXT. LOTUS RIVER / DEN UNDER LOTUS ROOTS - DAY**

*Magar carries Jambug across glittering water. Lotus roots tangle below like a secret room. The RIVERBANK WOMAN waits on the bank, hope and dread in her posture.*

RIVERBANK WOMAN  
Did you bring it?

*Jambug leaps lightly from Magar’s back onto a low root arching over **the den under the lotus roots** — a hollow of shade, leaves, and stored things.*

JAMBUG  
My heart is here, as promised. Safe where I keep what matters.

*He reaches into the den — not for a heart, but for a small bundle he cached: a pouch. He tosses it down. Coins spill in the mud: **the astrologer's fee**, stolen back in the night by a monkey who listens.*

JAMBUG (CONT’D)  
And here is **the astrologer's fee** he took for **the astrologer's false cure**. Count it. A lie has a price — and it should be returned.

*The woman stares at the coins, then at Jambug. Magar freezes, realization dawning.*

MAGAR  
Jambug… you never—

JAMBUG  
I never kept my heart anywhere but in my chest, where it beats for my friends. But I kept my wits in **the den under the lotus roots**. Magar, look at her house. Look at the water standing in her fields.

*Jambug points past the woman: a silted channel choked with reeds, flooding the threshold.*

JAMBUG (CONT’D)  
No heart cures a blocked channel. Only hands do.

*Magar turns from Jambug to the woman — the true remedy visible at last. The spell of the false cure breaks.*

RIVERBANK WOMAN *(voice breaking)*  
I asked you to kill for a lie.

MAGAR  
And I nearly carried my friend to it. No more.

CUT TO:

SC05
**EXT. RIVERBANK VILLAGE / LOTUS RIVER - DUSK**

*A warm dusk. Jambug, Magar, and the RIVERBANK WOMAN (now with her children helping) work together clearing the silted channel. Mud, laughter, effort. Water begins to flow freely again, draining the flooded threshold.*

RIVERBANK WOMAN  
The astrologer is gone with his shame. The water moves because we moved it.

MAGAR  
And because a friend refused to let me forget myself.

*Jambug presses the returned coins into the woman’s hands, then gently guides her to give them to her children.*

JAMBUG  
For rice, not for stars. **The mangoes traded daily** are sweeter when no one pays with a heart.

*The children cheer as the channel runs clear. The river glows with sunset.*

*Magar and Jambug return to the lotus water. Jambug tosses a mango; Magar catches it — the old ritual restored, wiser now.*

MAGAR  
Will you still come to the middle of the river?

JAMBUG  
Only if you promise never to shop for hearts again.

MAGAR *(a low, relieved laugh)*  
I promise. **The lotus river friendship** is not for sale.

*They drift together among pink blooms as temple bells ring and the first stars appear — loyalty held, wit used kindly, the nīti of the river intact.*

FADE OUT.
