# YS01 — The Fog That Changed the Air

**Category:** Historical event / science / public policy  
**Working title:** When London’s Fog Turned Deadly  
**Alternatives:** `The Week London Couldn’t See`; `The Great Smog: Five Days That Changed Britain`; `How London’s Coal Smoke Became a Killer Fog`  
**Premise:** In December 1952, a weather inversion trapped smoke and pollution over London. A familiar winter fog became a public health crisis, and the slow response helped push clean-air laws forward.

**Audience:** General viewers, 12+ accessible language; adults interested in history, cities, health, and policy.  
**Central curiosity:** How did a city mistake a dangerous pollution event for ordinary fog, and what finally changed?  
**Promised feeling:** Unease turning into understanding and a modest sense of agency.  
**Click reason:** The title turns a familiar sight into a threat and promises a clear historical cause.  
**Continuation reason:** Viewers learn in stages why the fog formed, why people initially misread it, what the death estimates do and do not say, and how public pressure became law.  
**Distinctive angle:** The story follows the invisible ingredients and slow public recognition, rather than treating the smog as a single shocking statistic.

## Packaging

- **Primary title:** When London’s Fog Turned Deadly
- **Alternative titles:** The Week London Couldn’t See; The Great Smog: Five Days That Changed Britain; How London’s Coal Smoke Became a Killer Fog
- **Thumbnail:** A London streetlamp swallowed by yellow-grey haze; a tiny figure beside it for scale. Large simple silhouette, no fake corpse or invented detail.
- **Thumbnail text:** `THE AIR TURNED`
- **Promise:** A real winter fog became dangerous because pollution and weather combined.
- **Opening must deliver:** Show how an ordinary fog could become deadly and name the December 1952 event immediately.
- **Evidence needed:** Weather and pollution mechanism; dates and duration; health-impact estimates with uncertainty; legislative timeline. Do not imply one exact death total is settled.
- **Credibility risks:** Mixing immediate deaths with later statistical estimates; implying legislation instantly cleaned the air; overclaiming individual causation.

## Hook options

1. “London’s fog was so thick that the city seemed to vanish. But the danger was not just that people could not see. They were breathing what the city had burned.”
2. “For five winter days, London breathed air that turned its own heating system against it.”

**Selected:** Hook 1. It begins with a clear image, then pivots to the cause without sensational figures.

## Concept selection note

Strong animation potential: map, weather layers, chimney smoke, street visibility, and a simple lung/particle diagram. Main risk is that a visual fog sequence could become repetitive; each return to the city must reveal a new consequence or fact.
