# YS08 concept — The Summer That Never Came

**Category:** Overlooked history / climate science
**Working title:** The Summer That Never Came
**Alternates:** Why 1816 Had Snow in Summer; The Volcano That Changed the Weather a World Away; When Summer Disappeared in 1816
**Premise:** After Mount Tambora erupted in 1815, fine particles and gases in the upper atmosphere helped cool parts of the world. In 1816, some regions faced summer frost, rain, failed harvests, and rising food prices.
**Audience:** General viewers interested in unusual history, weather, and science.
**Central curiosity:** How could an eruption in Indonesia help make summer cold thousands of miles away?
**Promised feeling:** Disorientation turning into a clear chain from eruption to atmospheric particles to regional hardship, with the limits of that chain visible.
**Click reason:** Snow and frost in a season meant for warmth.
**Continuation reason:** The viewer follows an eruption, delayed atmospheric effects, uneven regional weather, and human consequences.
**Distinctive angle:** Avoid claiming the entire world had no summer; focus on documented impacts in parts of North America and western/central Europe.
**Evidence/research:** UCAR Center for Science Education, peer-reviewed Luterbacher and Pfister (2015), NPS chronology; distinguish volcanic cooling from local weather and avoid sweeping famine claims.
**Thumbnail:** A summer calendar with snow on a field and a small globe route from Indonesia. Text `JUNE. SNOW.`
**Credibility risks:** Overstating global uniformity, using inaccurate temperature figures, making unsupported causal claims about cholera or Frankenstein, or suggesting climate lasted permanently.
**Selection screen:** Strong visual cause-and-effect and historical stakes; scope can remain clear by contrasting only documented regions. Selected for batch 4.
