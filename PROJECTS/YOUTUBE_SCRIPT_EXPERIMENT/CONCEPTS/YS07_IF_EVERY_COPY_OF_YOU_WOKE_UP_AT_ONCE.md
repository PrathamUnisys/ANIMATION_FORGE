# YS07 concept — If Every Copy of You Woke Up at Once

**Category:** Philosophy / hypothetical thought experiment
**Working title:** If Every Copy of You Woke Up at Once
**Alternates:** Which One Is the Real You?; If Your Mind Was Copied Twice, Who Would You Be?; The Copy Problem That Breaks the Idea of “You”
**Premise:** A fictional machine makes two people who remember your life and feel like you. The story changes one detail at a time to expose why memory, body, and personal identity do not give an easy answer.
**Audience:** General viewers interested in identity, science fiction, and philosophy.
**Central curiosity:** If two future people both remember being you, which one—if either—is you?
**Promised feeling:** Curiosity becoming a genuine dilemma, then an open but satisfying distinction between identity and what we care about.
**Click reason:** A simple copying machine creates an impossible choice.
**Continuation reason:** Each version changes one fact—one copy, two copies, original body stays—so the answer keeps shifting.
**Distinctive angle:** The script marks the machine as fiction and treats the question as philosophy, not a prediction about current technology.
**Evidence/research:** Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy on personal identity, psychological continuity, and fission; Derek Parfit’s teletransporter thought experiment as primary philosophical context.
**Thumbnail:** One silhouette facing two identical lit doorways; text `WHICH ONE IS YOU?`
**Credibility risks:** Presenting a disputed philosophical issue as solved; confusing a copy’s memories with evidence of survival; implying real mind-copying technology exists.
**Selection screen:** High-concept, low-cost visuals, strong interactive question; risk of abstraction managed through a single recurring personal scenario. Selected for batch 4.
