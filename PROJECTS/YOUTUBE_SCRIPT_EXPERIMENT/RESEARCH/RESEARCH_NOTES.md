# Research notes

Research date: 2026-11-10. This is a working evidence log, not a claim that any structure guarantees views.

## Platform guidance

- YouTube says packaging should accurately represent the video, be concise, and match the audience. Its official help describes searchable and intriguing title approaches and recommends checking impressions and click-through in Analytics. [YouTube: Thumbnail & title tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300?hl=en)
- YouTube describes performance in terms of appeal, engagement, and satisfaction, and recommends that intros quickly deliver the value promised by the title and thumbnail. [YouTube: Understand content performance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559650?hl=en)
- YouTube's current title/thumbnail testing guidance uses watch-time share to choose winners and acknowledges same/inconclusive results. Packaging review alone cannot establish a winner. [YouTube: A/B test titles & thumbnails](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en)
- YouTube's audience-retention report is video-level evidence. It defines the intro measure at 30 seconds and notes that dips/spikes can have more than one interpretation (skip/leave, rewatch/share, or confusion). It recommends comparing a video against the creator's similar-length history. This is the measurement method for later real-world tests, not a prediction we can make from scripts. [YouTube: Measure key moments for audience retention](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en)
- YouTube describes audience response through appeal, engagement, and satisfaction. This supports keeping package accuracy and viewer experience in view together; it does not show that any one script structure produces them. [YouTube: Content tab analytics tips](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?hl=en)

## Public video examples studied for craft (not as performance evidence)

These are examples of visible choices in published videos and creator materials. A public view count or a polished video does not reveal its private retention graph, and these examples do not establish that their structures caused success.

| Area | Example | Craft observation used as a question for this experiment |
|---|---|---|
| Mystery / investigative documentary | LEMMiNO, [The Search For D. B. Cooper](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CbUjuwhQPKs) | Its public chapter sequence moves from hijacking to manhunt, money, leap, and suspects. We ask whether ordered evidence turns can sustain a mystery without constant cliffhangers. Chapters are not retention data. |
| Investigative explanation | Johnny Harris, [Why People Think the Government Killed JFK](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2r5eKpptixo) | The title promises an explanation of a belief, not a declaration that the belief is true. It is a useful packaging distinction for contested subjects. |
| Science / educational entertainment | Veritasium, [The Most Misunderstood Concept in Physics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DxL2HoqLbyA) | A broad claim creates a clear curiosity promise; the script still has to earn the claim through explanation. We do not borrow the title's superlative for claims without a defined basis. |
| History | Fall of Civilizations, [The Bronze Age Collapse — Mediterranean Apocalypse](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B965f8AcNbw) | A large historical puzzle is framed as a named event and geography, giving viewers a way into a complex subject. The dramatic subtitle needs evidence and scope. |
| Psychology / human behaviour | Veritasium, [The Stanford Prison Experiment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KND_bBDE8RQ) | The video challenges a familiar interpretation. It is a model for making a claim arguable and then examining it; the experiment itself is contested and must not be treated as uncomplicated authority. |
| Real-world survival story | The Infographics Show, [Insane Way A Man Survived 76 Days Lost At Sea](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=exqELqiqSmM) | Public chapter list separates several survival accounts. We ask whether one clear human problem can be followed through changing obstacles; the channel's presentation is not a source for YS06's historical facts. |
| Hypothetical / speculative | Kurzgesagt, [What If We Detonated All Nuclear Bombs at Once?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JyECrGp-Sw8) | A single, bounded “what if” creates a visible question. Our YS07 uses a fictional thought experiment but labels it as such and does not imply technical feasibility. |
| Creator process | Kurzgesagt, [About our channel and process](https://kurzgesagt.org/what-we-do?visit=videos) | The team describes research, source sheets, and a balance between facts and story. This supports source transparency as production practice, not a guaranteed retention trick. |
| Creator interview | Johnny Harris, [How he writes YouTube videos](https://usecaddie.app/s/johnny-harris-reveals-how-he-writes-youtube) | Interview topics include promise, paradox, retention, and making complexity simple. This is self-reported craft advice, useful for generating hypotheses, not independent evidence of causal impact. |

**Evidence classes:** YouTube Help pages are platform guidance; the linked videos are qualitative examples; creator interviews are self-reported practice; the experiment's script reviews are editorial judgments; retention hypotheses remain untested until videos are published and measured.

## Editorial principles used in batch 1

The script choices below are hypotheses: a concrete opening image, a legible central question, causal discoveries, and an ending that changes how the opening is understood may help viewers follow a story. The scripts need audience testing before any retention claim can be made.
