## Verdict

The script handles regional differences and causal limits better than a simple “volcano cooled the world” story. The cause-to-consequence chain is clear, but some links need stronger sourcing and the ending repeats the framing rather than adding a distinct final insight.

## Findings

- **NOTE — Package fit:** “The Summer That Never Came” and `JUNE. SNOW.` are compelling and recognizable. The script correctly qualifies the phrase as non-literal. The alternative “The Volcano That Changed the Weather a World Away” is broad but consistent with the premise.
- **NOTE — Opening, 00:00:** June snow, damaged crops, and a faraway volcano create a clear question quickly. The sentence “The volcano blamed for this strange summer” sounds as if blame is disputed; the later script states a contribution with care. Consider saying “linked to” or naming Tambora directly to avoid ambiguity.
- **MINOR — Cause explanation, 01:15–02:50:** The distinction between nearby ash and high-altitude particles is useful. “Some of those gases formed tiny particles” is accessible, but does not identify sulfur aerosols; a brief plain-language label would help viewers connect the particle veil to the scientific explanation. Attribute the claim that those particles reduced sunlight to the cited research.
- **MINOR — Quantitative clarity, 02:50:** The “two to four degrees Celsius below a later historical reference average” claim is appropriately regional, but the narration and on-screen note should name the comparison period (the cited paper uses 1951–1980) and specify that it applies to western/central Europe and the western Mediterranean. Without that, the number can sound like a global temperature drop.
- **MINOR — Human consequence, 04:40–06:30:** The crop-to-price explanation is coherent but remains general. The script explicitly labels the family as representative, which is honest; add a sourced individual account or a clearly marked example to give this section a human anchor. Do not use a composite as if it were a documented family.
- **NOTE — Causal boundaries, 06:30:** The script rightly warns against attributing every hardship to Tambora. Keep the distinction between the eruption’s broad cooling contribution and the role of regional weather, food storage, trade, and local vulnerability. “The particles gradually left the atmosphere” could be more precise about the timescale and source; avoid implying all effects ended on one date.
- **MINOR — Ending, 08:00:** “A distant event can alter the conditions people depend on” restates the premise from the middle. The final line about a summer failing to act like summer is memorable, but the paragraph could be shortened so it does not repeat the same lesson in three forms.
- **NOTE — Spoken clarity:** “Climate is the longer pattern those daily conditions build together” is a little abstract for first listen. A more direct weather/climate distinction would be easier to follow. Avoid using “background conditions” without an immediate concrete referent.
- **NOTE — Production feasibility:** A global route map, atmosphere cross-section, regional temperature map, and crop-market sequence are feasible with simple animation. Keep regional shading distinct; a uniform blue world map would contradict the script’s caveat.
- **NOTE — Factual support and runtime:** The sources are credible, including the peer-reviewed Luterbacher and Pfister paper. Check every claim about local crop damage, grain prices, and food access against a source before recording. Runtime is correctly unmeasured but pending word count; calculate actual narration words and state the pace/holds used for the `SCRIPT_ESTIMATE`.

## Promise and payoff

The title is deliberately non-literal, and the opening quickly explains that regional weather varied. The thumbnail and title fit the script. The ending answers how the distant eruption affected some regions, but its final insight would be stronger if it focused on one specific consequence already established rather than restating the broad cause-to-consequence idea.
