# YS09 Independent Second Review — Revised Script

**Review status:** Independent current-version review. I read the revised script and its source notes, not the original review, change report, or writer self-assessment. This is a separate critic review, not self-review. No audience performance data is available or claimed.

## Overall verdict

**Verdict: Strong, clear explainer with one minor deliverable inconsistency.** The script follows a message from phone to seabed cable, then uses the March 2024 West Africa outage to make the redundancy question concrete. The explanation is understandable for a general audience, the outage is appropriately qualified, and the ending answers the opening question. The title is accurate, though the primary package is more descriptive than curiosity-led.

## Findings

### MINOR — Thumbnail sells the infrastructure more than the story question

**Evidence:** Header thumbnail at the top of the file: “A phone above a dark ocean, with a glowing cable linking two coasts”; text `UNDER THE INTERNET`. The primary title is “The Cables Beneath the Internet.”

The image and title clearly identify the subject, but neither signals the break-and-redundancy puzzle that the promise and opening deliver. The alternate “How the Internet Survives Undersea Cable Cuts” communicates that payoff more directly. Consider showing a severed route alongside a surviving path, or use the more specific alternate title. The current package is accurate, but its click question is less legible at a glance.

### MINOR — Stale count/runtime instruction contradicts the completed metadata

**Evidence:** End of “Source notes,” final claims-to-verify paragraph: “Runtime and narration count: To be recalculated after revision,” despite the header listing **860 narration words** and **8:59–9:09**.

This is a document consistency defect. Remove or update the stale instruction so production users do not mistake the completed figures for pending work.

## Review by criterion

- **Opening and first 30 seconds:** The opening immediately delivers a vivid physical image and a scoped statistic (“More than 99 percent of international data flows”), then asks the core question. It pays off the title without unnecessary setup. The statistic is explicitly limited to international data flows in both narration and source notes.
- **Progression and retention:** The sequence at 00:00, 01:00, 02:20, 03:45, 05:08, and 06:40 has a useful escalation: route → packets → cable → real outage → limits of redundancy → repair. The 03:45 event changes the stakes, and 05:08 qualifies the tempting “many cables means safe” conclusion. No major middle section feels repetitive.
- **Narration and accessibility:** Router, packet, landing station, and network capacity are explained with familiar comparisons or visual support. Sentence lengths vary naturally. “A line on a world map can look like a backup” is an effective plain-language description of the distinction between mapped routes and usable capacity.
- **Credibility:** The script preserves the suspected status of the 2024 rock slide and distinguishes degraded service from near-total outages. The Internet Society report supports the four affected cable systems, the varying country impacts, and the shared convergence area; Ghana’s regulator supports all four links to Europe being out and use of regional routes/local caches. The ITU statistic is correctly scoped. Sources: [ITU Global Connectivity Report 2025](https://www.itu.int/itu-d/reports/statistics/global-connectivity-report-2025/), [Internet Society 2024 West Africa report](https://www.internetsociety.org/resources/doc/2024/2024-west-africa-submarine-cable-outage-report/), [Ghana NCA update](https://nca.org.gh/2024/03/15/update-3-undersea-cable-disruptions-affect-data-services/).
- **Ending:** At 08:10, “One broken cable can matter. It just does not tell the whole story” resolves the opening question without promising uninterrupted service. **The ending delivers on the opening and title promise.**
- **Production feasibility:** Maps, packet animation, a cable cutaway, a route-convergence diagram, and a repair-ship sequence are all feasible with modest 2D/3D assets. Visual instructions reinforce the explanation and avoid expensive spectacle.
- **Count/runtime/timecodes:** I ran the repository narration counter against the current revised file: **860 words**. At 115 wpm, narration alone is about **7:29**; adding the stated **90–100 seconds** of visual-only holds gives approximately **8:59–9:09**, matching the header. Runtime is properly labelled `SCRIPT_ESTIMATE`; it is not a measured recording or animatic. The Retention Map timecodes match the script chapter headings.
