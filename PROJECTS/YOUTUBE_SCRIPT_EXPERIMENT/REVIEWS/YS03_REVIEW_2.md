# YS03 Revised — Independent Second-Pass Review

**Review scope:** Revised script and its included title, thumbnail, audience, and runtime package. This is an independent editorial review, distinct from the writer's self-assessment. It is not an audience test, animatic review, or approval.

## Verdict

**Overall: Strong, clear natural-history story with a coherent promise and feasible visuals.** The package gives viewers a specific puzzle; the script corrects the misleading “turned to stone” impression early, explains the preservation process, and ends on the contrast between remains and living flamingos. The main weakness is that the closing restates a contrast already made moments earlier. The runtime is a planning estimate and its arithmetic is off by about one second at each boundary.

## Review

### Title and thumbnail

The title, **“The Lake That Makes Dead Animals Look Like Statues,”** is clear and supported by the story. The thumbnail phrase **“STONE OR SALT?”** creates a readable question that the script answers. The package's use of “makes” is slightly simplified because the photographer posed some of the already-dead remains; the opening addresses this promptly, so the overall promise is fair.

### First 30 seconds and retention

At **00:00**, “These birds look like stone statues” opens on the visual promise, then immediately corrects the false inference that living animals were turned to stone. This is a strong hook: the correction adds a question without withholding the basic facts. The script then gives the viewer two connected lines of inquiry: how remains are preserved and why flamingos live there.

The sequence from image context (**01:05**) to lake chemistry (**02:35**), mineral coating (**04:20**), and flamingo habitat (**06:00**) is easy to follow. The turns are meaningful rather than arbitrary. The pH explanation is accessible, though the chemistry section carries several facts in succession; the basin and pH visuals should help maintain orientation.

### Narration and accessibility

The language is generally direct and suitable for first-listen comprehension. Terms such as “alkalinity,” “pH,” and “calcified” receive enough context or correction. The narration avoids a horror tone and does not overstate the danger to all wildlife.

**MINOR — Repeated ending image, 06:00 and 07:45:** The 06:00 section says, “So the two images belong together: mineral-coated remains on the shore, and living birds using the lake to raise the next generation.” At 07:45 it repeats, “Those two pictures belong together: mineral-coated remains on the shore, and living birds crossing the red water.” The final callback is effective, but the near-duplicate setup weakens the final beat. Shorten the earlier recap or make the last image add a new detail.

### Evidence and credibility

The script appropriately says the pictured animals were already dead and avoids assigning a cause of death to each specimen. The Smithsonian account supports the photographer's description that he posed carcasses in lifelike positions; NASA supports the mud temperature and flamingo nesting-barrier claims. The distinctions between lake conditions and species adaptation are responsibly phrased.

**MINOR — Scope of the opening, 00:00:** “They were then placed in lifelike poses for the camera” can sound as if every animal in the famous images was posed. Later passages correctly narrow this to “some” remains. Keep that qualification in the opening too.

### Production feasibility

The proposed basin diagram, pH dial, map, reconstructed still-life setup, and wide lake transition are feasible with modest 2D/3D animation. The script correctly labels the staged-photo sequence as a reconstruction and cautions against depicting an unknown cause of death. The planned 75–85 seconds of holds are substantial but can be produced without complex simulation.

### Runtime

The runtime is correctly labelled `SCRIPT_ESTIMATE` and explicitly says it has not been measured through narration or animatic. At 763 words and 105 words per minute, narration is about **7:16**; adding 75–85 seconds gives about **8:31–8:41**, versus the listed **8:30–8:40**. This is a small arithmetic mismatch. Recalculate after the holds are placed on a timeline.

## Packaging and ending

**Packaging fit:** Yes. The title, thumbnail, and opening point to the same question, and the script answers it without claiming living animals become stone.

**Does the ending fulfill the opening?** Yes. The closing returns to the image and pairs preserved remains with living flamingos, resolving both parts of the opening promise. The final section could be tighter because the same contrast is stated at 06:00.
