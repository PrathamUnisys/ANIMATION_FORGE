# YS07 Original — Independent Review 1

**Script:** `SCRIPTS/ORIGINAL/YS07_IF_EVERY_COPY_OF_YOU_WOKE_UP_AT_ONCE.md`  
**Reviewer:** Separate critic agent; independent of the writer.  
**Scope:** Title/thumbnail/audience/runtime context and original narration only. I did not consult concept assessments, revision claims, or other reviews.  
**Status:** Editorial review only; no score, approval, or performance claim.

## Verdict

The thought experiment is easy to picture and develops its central puzzle in a clear order. The strongest opportunity is to make the abstract section on identity and survival more concrete, while keeping philosophical positions distinct from the script’s preferred ending.

## Findings

- **NOTE — Package fit:** “If Every Copy of You Woke Up at Once” and `WHICH ONE IS YOU?` clearly promise a branching identity puzzle. “Every copy” is broader than the single two-copy case in the script; either align the title to two copies or make the title’s general case explicit.
- **NOTE — Opening, 00:00:** “You wake up in a room with two people who look exactly like you” gives an immediate image and choice. “You can only let one leave” raises an extra coercive problem that the script never explains. Clarify who imposes this choice or remove it and let the central identity question carry the hook.
- **MINOR — Concept clarity, 01:10:** “Two shirts can look the same without being one shirt” is accessible, but the script moves from resemblance to identity and then to a continuing body/mind without an ordinary example of why the distinction matters. A simple example such as sleep, memory loss, or gradual body change could help a listener follow the question before the scanner appears.
- **NOTE — Fiction boundary, 02:40:** The script clearly labels the scanner as fictional. Keep the graphic disclaimer visible; avoid visuals that suggest present-day technology can copy a mind.
- **MINOR — Retention and progression, 02:40–06:20:** The one-copy and two-copy stages build logically. The section at **06:20** introduces several positions, Parfit, relationships, responsibility, and memory in one run. A concrete example of a promise remembered by both copies would reduce cognitive load and give that section an emotional stake.
- **MINOR — Philosophical boundaries, 06:20–08:20:** “A third view warns that no single simple test always settles who continues” is vague. Name the view or state that philosophers disagree about whether bodily continuity, mental continuity, or another relation matters. At **08:20**, “The simplest ending may be that the copies are two new people” is a useful framing, but it should remain clearly the script’s proposed interpretation rather than a settled answer.
- **NOTE — Narration clarity:** Most language is conversational. “Personal identity,” “psychological continuity,” and “branching” should be briefly explained when spoken; the draft explains the last but could make the first two still plainer. “The machine treated them exactly alike” is clear and effective.
- **NOTE — Ending:** Returning to the two doorways pays off the opening image. The final question is meaningful, though it expands into a broad question about survival; consider ending on a short callback to the two people choosing separate lives.
- **NOTE — Production feasibility:** Two matching figures, a scanner, and branching paths are feasible with simple animation. Avoid implying facial/body sameness proves memory or consciousness; label the story as hypothetical.
- **NOTE — Runtime:** `SCRIPT_ESTIMATE` is correctly marked unmeasured, but the file says count pending. Calculate spoken-word count and pace-based estimate before production planning. No actual retention data exists.

## Promise and payoff

Title and thumbnail accurately signal the identity puzzle, with the “every copy” scope caveat above. The script returns to its opening question and leaves a philosophical question rather than claiming scientific proof. Ending delivers, but can feel less abstract with one concrete final beat.
