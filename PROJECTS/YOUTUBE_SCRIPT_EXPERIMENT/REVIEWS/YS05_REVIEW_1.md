# YS05 Original — Independent Review 1

**Script:** `SCRIPTS/ORIGINAL/YS05_THE_BRIDGE_THAT_BEGAN_TO_TWIST.md`  
**Reviewer:** Separate critic agent; independent of the writer.  
**Scope:** Packaging and complete original narration. I did not consult concept assessments, prior reviews, or revision claims.  
**Status:** Editorial review only. No approval or audience-performance claim.

## Verdict

Strong visual mystery and a useful engineering explanation, but the collapse chronology needs correction before narration. The mechanism explanation also needs a tighter distinction between the broadly accepted findings and the exact start of the destructive motion.

## Findings

- **MINOR — Click promise:** The primary title, “The Bridge That Began to Twist,” and thumbnail `WHY THE TWIST?` accurately promise the central question. The alternatives are less even: “The Bridge That Wind Could Tear Apart” sounds sensational, and “Galloping Gertie” may be unfamiliar to casual viewers.
- **NOTE — Opening, 00:00:** “The bridge was not simply bouncing” and the change from rise-and-fall to twist quickly establish a visual question. The claim “within minutes, sections of the bridge were breaking apart and falling” compresses the timeline in a misleading way: the narration later places the first concrete loss at about 11, nearly an hour after the twist began.
- **MAJOR — Event chronology, 02:30–04:30:** The script says the deck began twisting “shortly after ten,” then says “At around eleven, pieces of concrete and steel began to break away.” WSDOT’s chronology places the twist at 10:03, first concrete falling at 10:03, a large chunk at about 10:30, and the center-span section breaking free at 11:02; the collapse was over by 11:10. Correct the sequence and distinguish first damage from final failure. [WSDOT chronology](https://wsdot.wa.gov/TNBhistory/collapse.htm)
- **MINOR — Human beat, 02:30:** Coatsworth’s escape and Tubby’s loss provide a strong human anchor, but the section moves quickly from the attempted rescue to generalized failure. A short, sourced beat about why rescuers could not reach the car would make the danger more legible. Avoid inventing Tubby’s thoughts or a precise emotional reaction.
- **MINOR — Mechanism clarity, 04:30:** The playground-swing analogy is simple, but “the bridge’s own movement helped change how the wind pushed it” remains abstract. Use one clear sequence: deck turns → airflow/forces change → forces feed the twist. The source also describes excessive flexibility, solid girders acting like an airfoil, and a cable-band slip associated with the pivotal transition; the script should not imply that a single exact trigger is settled. [WSDOT failure analysis](https://wsdot.wa.gov/TNBhistory/bridges-failure.htm)
- **NOTE — Uncertainty, 06:30:** The distinction between the main explanation and debated details is responsible. “The broader lesson is clear” risks making uncertainty sound fully resolved; name precisely what is well supported and what remains disputed.
- **MINOR — Ending, 08:00:** “The next time you see a long bridge, imagine the air moving around its deck” shifts to generic audience instruction. The ending would be stronger if it returned to the actual Tacoma Narrows lesson: test how wind and a flexible deck interact before construction.
- **NOTE — Production feasibility:** Bridge diagrams, wave/twist comparison, wind-tunnel model, and a restrained collapse timeline are all feasible for a small animation team. Any reconstruction should be labelled schematic; do not imply exact camera views beyond the cited historical film.
- **NOTE — Runtime evidence:** `SCRIPT_ESTIMATE` is properly marked as unmeasured. The approximate 8–10 minute range should be replaced with a verified narration word count and pace-based estimate; no performance measurement is available.

## Packaging and ending

The title and thumbnail accurately represent the script’s central motion puzzle. The ending answers why wind mattered and what engineers learned, but it currently resolves on a broad viewer prompt rather than the specific design change. No measured click or retention data exists.
