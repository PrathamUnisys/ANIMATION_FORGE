# GENRE INSTRUCTION — COMEDY

## Genre identity and promise

Comedy aims to create amusement through character, situation, language, contrast, surprise, incongruity or visual action. The project brief should define the intended comic flavor: character comedy, situational comedy, satire, absurdism, parody, slapstick, dark comedy, wordplay or a blend.

Do not assume comedy means every line must contain a joke. Contrast, sincerity, tension and silence often make the funny moments work.

## Comic engine

Define the repeatable source of comedy for the story or series. Examples include a character's overconfidence, an impossible bureaucracy, mismatched goals, literal interpretations, social status reversals or a world whose rules create absurd outcomes.

A comic engine should generate fresh situations while remaining understandable. Do not repeat the same gag without escalation or a new consequence.

## Setups, payoffs and reversals

- Establish the setup clearly enough that the audience can register it.
- Make payoffs surprising but fair in hindsight.
- Use callbacks when they gain meaning or escalate.
- Let reversals arise from character choices and established world rules.
- Give physical comedy readable preparation, action and reaction.
- Use the strongest reveal, reaction or visual beat as the timing anchor.

## Characters and relationships

Build humor from character-specific behavior, wants, flaws, skills and relationships. Characters should not all joke in the same voice. Preserve emotional truth even when characters behave absurdly.

Avoid making a character's only trait their incompetence, accent, appearance or identity. The audience should understand what each character wants, not merely watch random foolishness.

## Dialogue and voice

Keep exchanges purposeful. Use contrast, subtext, status games, interruption, understatement, over-explanation, misdirection and carefully placed pauses. Avoid explaining a joke after it lands. A character's literal spoken words must remain separate from performance cues and timing metadata.

## Visual comedy

Use staging, composition, silhouette, scale, prop behavior, anticipation, reaction and consequence. A gag must read visually without relying on an unfilmable explanation. Reactions are often as important as the action.

## Structure and pacing

Alternate tension, release and escalation. Do not stack punchlines so densely that the audience cannot process them. Ensure scenes still advance character, conflict or plot. A serious beat can strengthen comedy when the shift is earned.

## Common failure modes

- Random jokes unrelated to character or story.
- Repeating a gag without escalation.
- Explaining or overplaying punchlines.
- Characters sharing the same comic voice.
- Confusing cruelty with wit.
- A joke undermining a high-stakes moment before the audience understands the stakes.
- Physical action that is unclear, unsafe to stage or too expensive for its payoff.

## Production implications

For each visual gag, record required props, positions, timing, anticipation, contact/reaction and reset state. Identify recurring props and locations for reuse. Break down dialogue timing, pauses, reactions and sound cues separately. Choose animation and audio tools from the tool catalogue only after actual requirements are known.

## Screenplay review checklist

- [ ] The comic engine is easy to explain.
- [ ] Humor emerges from character, situation or established rules.
- [ ] Setups and payoffs are traceable.
- [ ] Dialogue voices are distinct.
- [ ] Physical gags are visually readable.
- [ ] Timing includes space for audience comprehension.
- [ ] Emotional and plot beats survive the jokes.
- [ ] Production complexity is justified by the comic payoff.

## Interaction with secondary genres and channel style

Comedy may be primary or secondary. When secondary, it adds comic moments without turning every dramatic beat into a joke. A channel style guide may set humor density, episode pacing and recurring segments, but it cannot replace the story's comic engine or flatten every character into the same channel voice.
