# Script Creation Framework — Research Report

**Research date:** 2026-11-10  
**Status:** Research-backed design input. This is not a claim that any script method guarantees views or retention.

## Research question and method

Question: how can a small creator turn a starting idea into a compelling, factually responsible, visually producible YouTube documentary/explainer script without forcing all subjects into the same formula?

Method: inspect Animation Forge's current story, research, review, QC and runtime guidance; read the ten original/revised scripts, all twenty reviews, change reports, research notes, cross-script report and proposal; search current official YouTube creator guidance and primary/authoritative journalism and science-communication sources. Public videos and creator interviews in the prior experiment are treated as craft examples or self-reported practice, never as causal retention evidence.

## Evidence classes

### Platform guidance (verified on research date)

- YouTube describes performance through viewer appeal, engagement and satisfaction; its retention report is available at the video level. The 30-second intro metric reflects the percentage still watching after 30 seconds. Dips and spikes are ambiguous: a spike may reflect interest, replay/sharing, or confusion, so the graph points to a passage to inspect rather than explaining why behavior occurred. [YouTube retention guide](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415?hl=en-GB)
- YouTube's current title/thumbnail experiment tool compares up to three variants and selects by watch-time share. It may report same or inconclusive; platform guidance says a high CTR alone can differ from a watch-time-oriented winner. Packaging review can assess honesty and clarity, but cannot pick an empirically best package before audience testing. [YouTube A/B test guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en-GB)
- The platform guidance describes what the creator can measure; it does not establish a universal hook, script length, structure, or “viral” formula.

### Journalism and documentary standards

- The Society of Professional Journalists advises verification before release, use of original sources when possible, context, and clear corrections. These are professional ethical standards, not an experimental proof of storytelling impact. [SPJ Code of Ethics](https://www.spj.org/spj-code-of-ethics/)
- Reuters' published standards prefer named sources where possible, retain responsibility for accuracy, and caution against asking sources to approve a full story in a way that lets them materially reshape it. This supports retaining source identity and independent editorial control. [Reuters standards and values](https://reutersagency.com/about/standards-values/)
- PBS's accuracy standards say producers should be able to identify a source for each asserted fact and why it is reliable, and should consider misleading framing and false inferences, not only literal truth. [PBS accuracy standards](https://www.pbs.org/standards/accuracy/)
- BBC guidance says factual reconstructions should not mislead or sensationalize and should be based on substantial verifiable evidence and visibly identified. This is relevant to animation that depicts real events. [BBC accuracy guidance](https://www.bbc.co.uk/editorialguidelines/guidelines/accuracy/)

### Peer-reviewed communication research

- A 2025 study randomly assigned a representative sample of U.S. adults to four short documentary science films. Narrative engagement was associated with factual knowledge, interest and science-related outcomes; the paper also describes limitations, including correlational relationships between engagement and several outcomes. This is encouraging evidence that storytelling and learning can coexist in science films, but it does not identify one winning YouTube structure or prove causality for every outcome. [Howell et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1177/10755470241269885)
- Two experiments on hybrid television documentaries found better learning for educational facts tied closely to the narrative plotline than facts distant from it. This supports testing whether explanation is connected to the story's actual progression rather than inserted as detached exposition. The sample and medium are not YouTube scripts, so this is a design clue, not a guaranteed outcome. [Glaser, Garsoffky & Schwan, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.learninstruc.2011.05.006)
- A Royal Society Open Science paper explains that “uncertainty” can refer to different things—such as the hypothesis, estimate, or evidence quality—and that communicators should identify what is uncertain and why. This supports claim-level uncertainty notes instead of a vague “sources disagree” label. [van der Bles et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.181870)

### Prior experiment observations (not causal evidence)

Across the ten drafts, reviewers repeatedly asked for narrower title claims, a clear causal bridge, source-specific attribution, less repeated explanation, a better orientation sentence, and fresh word-count/runtime/map checks. The ten different story engines also support configurable methods. These are observations about this portfolio under editorial review; no published-video data exists.

## Design conclusions

1. Start with what is interesting and knowable, not an assumed act structure. Decide whether the strongest route is question-led, event-led, investigation-led, mechanism-led, person-led, conflict-led, place-led, or a bounded hypothetical.
2. Separate discovery from selection. Research should establish evidence and uncertainty first; concepts should then choose a truthful story focus. Packaging must match the selected story.
3. Treat each factual assertion as traceable. A script's sources section is helpful, but a claim ledger can show which lines each source supports and whether the source supports the same scope, time period, geography and causal wording.
4. Distinguish a source, a claim, a creative inference, a reconstruction and a hypothesis. AI-generated wording is never itself a source.
5. A narration script must work aloud and on first listen. Use simple everyday English while preserving mature themes and complex ideas; explain necessary technical terms without treating adult viewers as children.
6. Visual directions should carry explanation or emotional meaning. A diagram or analogy must not silently become evidence or imply exact reconstruction where it is only schematic.
7. Independent creative review and factual verification are separate. Reviewers cite exact passages; the fact checker works against source evidence; deterministic checks handle fields and cross-references; a human decides acceptance.
8. Revisions preserve the prior version and log affected passages/artifacts. Exact word count, runtime estimate, title and map should be recalculated or reconciled after every relevant edit.
9. Retention maps describe intended questions and payoffs. They are editorial hypotheses, not retention measurements.
10. Test real viewer outcomes only after publication. Compare truthful package variants with platform tools where available, and interpret analytics as observations with context, not universal laws.

## Methods considered and rejected as universal rules

- “Hook within X seconds,” mandatory cliffhangers, fixed reveal intervals, pattern interrupts every N seconds: no reliable evidence here supports universal thresholds; reject as gates.
- Universal three-act structure: reject. A three-act map can be an option, but it can distort a mechanism explainer, investigative question, philosophical thought experiment, or staged survival story.
- Viral title templates or guaranteed curiosity gaps: reject. The evidence supports accurate packaging and real-world testing, not guaranteed outcomes.
- Single numerical engagement score/average threshold: reject. Existing review schema conflicts with no-score guidance and the ten-script data cannot calibrate a score.
- Script-length targets as a substitute for timing: reject. Word count is only one runtime input; holds, diagrams, silence and delivery matter.

## Remaining unknowns

The repository experiment cannot tell us which concepts earn clicks, which hooks retain viewers, whether a particular analogy improves comprehension, how a given narrator performs, which edit produces satisfaction, or whether the proposed workflow is faster/better than the existing process. These require recorded scripts, viewer research or published analytics. A pilot can test completeness, factual traceability, usability, review quality and production feasibility, not viral performance.
