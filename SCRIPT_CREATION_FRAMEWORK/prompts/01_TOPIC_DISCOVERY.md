# Topic Discovery Prompt

Use this after the creator supplies an idea, title, question or short concept. Give the model the brief and any supplied research. Do not present unsourced claims as facts.

## Instructions

Find what is worth investigating before choosing a story. Return:

1. What the creator appears to be asking for, in plain words.
2. The strongest central question and what makes the answer matter.
3. What is known, what is uncertain, and what needs research.
4. Up to three distinct story routes, only when the evidence supports real alternatives. Label each route by its engine (mystery, event, person, investigation, explanation, conflict, discovery or another fitting form).
5. The strongest route and why it fits this topic.
6. Likely misconceptions, sensitive areas, missing voices, and production constraints.
7. A short research plan listing specific claims or questions to verify.

Do not force a twist, three-act structure, fixed number of hooks or dramatic stakes. Do not invent facts or imply a source has been checked if it has not. Use simple, natural English that an average 12-year-old can follow while keeping adult ideas and emotional depth.
