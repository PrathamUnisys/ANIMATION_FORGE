# Research Workflow

**Status:** Proposed manual-first workflow; no automated source discovery or claim verification is currently implemented here.

## 1. Define the research question

Record topic, intended story scope, key audience question, time/place/population bounds, source constraints and publication context. Break compound questions into claims that can be checked.

## 2. Find and preserve sources

Prefer original records, official datasets, legislation, direct publications, peer-reviewed research and primary documents where appropriate. Use authoritative secondary sources to add context, find leads and interpret technical evidence. Record source title, creator/publisher, date, URL, source class, locator (page/section/table/timecode), access date, and known conflicts or limitations.

Avoid treating search snippets, AI answers, repeated unattributed statements or social-media reposts as sufficient support. Verify an original source where available.

## 3. Build the claim ledger

Create one row per factual assertion important to the story. Include claim ID, draft passage/beat, precise wording, support source IDs/locators, what the sources establish, scope, uncertainty, source disagreement, verification state and needed action. If evidence supports only a narrower statement, narrow the prose. If a claim cannot be verified, remove it, label it as disputed/speculative, or leave it out.

## 4. Separate fact from interpretation

Use explicit labels for documented fact, source interpretation, writer inference, hypothetical, reconstruction, composite, disputed claim and unknown. For historical reconstruction, do not invent dialogue, inner thoughts or exact staging without evidentiary support. For science, distinguish established finding, model, hypothesis, limits and open debate.

## 5. Check after revision

Every material revision can change attribution or claim scope. Recheck affected script passages against the ledger and preserve updated verifier/date. A source link alone is not verification.

## 6. Report limitations

State claims not checked, inaccessible sources, conflicting evidence, rights questions and weak/indirect support. Keep factual verification separate from rights clearance; both may need human expertise. Do not call a whole script fact-checked when load-bearing claims remain unresolved.
